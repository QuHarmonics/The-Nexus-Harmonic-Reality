## 1. Why RHI Belongs in the Fold-Pressure Taxonomy

The paper locks the correct boundary:

$$
\boxed{
\text{No pressure, no } H.
}
$$

Enumeration systems read a lattice. Algebraic folds encode and recover address. Fold-pressure systems actively stabilize under constraint while paying an exhaust debt.

The RHI runtime is not merely an answer generator. It is a recursive constrained correction system:

$$
Q
\rightarrow
C_Q
\rightarrow
B_i
\rightarrow
A_i^{(\text{task})}
\rightarrow
G
\rightarrow
\Psi/\Omega.
$$

Where:

- $Q$ is the prompt.
- $C_Q$ is the task-local contract or need-slot.
- $B_i$ are branch candidates such as construct, verify, repair, and counter.
- $A_i^{(\text{task})}$ are task-local audits.
- $G$ is the collapse gate.
- $\Psi$ is accepted collapse.
- $\Omega$ is residue, refusal, uncertainty, or failed collapse.

This makes RHI a strong computational candidate for fold-pressure geometry because it contains all five required eligibility components.

## 2. RHI Eligibility Test

The paper defines $H$-eligibility through a strict conjunction:

$$
E_H = F \wedge B \wedge R \wedge X \wedge P.
$$

For RHI, the mapping is:

| Eligibility Term | Paper Meaning | RHI Runtime Mapping |
|---|---|---|
| $F$ | Feedback | branch generation, audit, repair history, rerun after failed gates |
| $B$ | Constraint / Bottleneck | task profile, contract gate, payload validity, two-file output interface |
| $R$ | Recursive State Dependence | trace continuity, repair history, branch scoring memory, previous gate failures |
| $X$ | Exhaust / Residue | rejected branches, failed repairs, $\Omega$, discarded verbose payload, spec-echo rejection |
| $P$ | Phase-Lock / Stability Requirement | $\Psi/\Omega$ collapse gate, task-local profile lock, anti-polysemy lock |

Thus:

$$
E_H^{(\text{RHI})}
=
F_{\text{audit}}
\wedge
B_{\text{contract}}
\wedge
R_{\text{trace}}
\wedge
X_{\text{rejection}}
\wedge
P_{\Psi/\Omega}.
$$

RHI is therefore not an enumeration system.

It is also not merely an algebraic fold, because it discards branch candidates and must stabilize through a live accept/reject gate.

The runtime pays informational exhaust:

$$
X_{\text{RHI}}
=
\text{rejected branches}
+
\Omega\text{-states}
+
\text{discarded payload mass}
+
\text{failed semantic carriers}.
$$

This makes it a practical AI substrate for measuring fold pressure.

$$

\frac{\|C_t\|}

where:

- $R_t$ is retained state, memory, stored structure, or historical momentum.
- $C_t$ is correction, update, intake, deformation, or fold response.
- $\epsilon$ prevents division by zero.

For RHI, the terminal-level observable can be framed as:

$$
R_t^{(\text{RHI})}
=
\text{accepted contract-consistent payload mass}
+
\text{retained trace}
+
\text{task-profile stability}

and:

$$
C_t^{(\text{RHI})}
=
\text{repair pressure}
+
\text{branch rejection pressure}

A direct canonical proxy is:

$$
H_{\text{RHI,obs}}

This is preferable to treating any raw output statistic as $H$.

The important rule remains:

## 4. Terminal RHI Metrics Already Available

The current RHI harness already supports terminal fold-pressure measurements.

Useful readouts include:

### 4.1 Residue Ratio

$$
H_{\Omega}
=
\frac{\#\Omega}{\#Q}.
$$

This measures how often the runtime refuses collapse or fails to find stable agreement.

### 4.2 Consensus Collapse Ratio

$$
H_{\text{consensus}}
=
\frac{\#\Psi_{\text{consensus}}}{\#\Psi}.
$$

This measures how often the runtime collapses through branch agreement rather than a single high-margin winner.

### 4.3 Exhaust Ratio

$$
H_{\text{exhaust}}
=
\frac{\#\text{rejected branches}}{\#\text{total branches}}.
$$

This measures the informational residue produced by the branch field.

### 4.4 Repair Success Ratio

$$
H_{\text{repair}}
=
\frac{\#(\text{repair attempts that become }\Psi)}
{\#\text{repair attempts}}.
$$

This measures whether corrective recursion actually stabilizes failed collapse paths.

### 4.5 Compression Ratio

For the payload shaper:

$$
H_{\text{compression}}
=
1
-
\frac{
\text{words}(\Psi_{\text{shaped}})
}{
\text{words}(\Psi_{\text{raw}})
}.
$$

This is the most direct language-level measure of:

$$
\text{high-dimensional potential}
\rightarrow
\text{low-dimensional structured payload}
+
\text{exhaust}.
$$

These are not all expected to equal $\pi/9$. They are fold-pressure telemetry channels. Only after many runs should one ask whether any channel clusters near:

$$
H=\frac{\pi}{9}\approx 0.34906585.

## 7. Schema Echo vs Requested Schema

A new refinement is required for the paper’s discussion of measurement rigor.

RHI originally punished schema-like output because earlier versions discovered the **spec echo problem**:

$$
\text{model repeats the scoring contract}
\rightarrow
\text{scorer mistakes echo for fit}.
$$

However, some prompts explicitly ask for a contract, checklist, verifier, or executable schema. In those cases, structured output is not necessarily spec echo.

Therefore the runtime needs a schema exception rule:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Schema output is penalized only when the prompt did not request schema.}
}
$$

Formally:

$$
P_{\text{schema-echo}}
=
\begin{cases}
0, & \text{if } Q \text{ requests contract/checklist/schema/verifier} \\
\lambda_{\text{echo}}, & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

This prevents false $\Omega$ outcomes where the model produced a valid structured contract but the anti-echo gate punished it for being structured.

## 9. Passive Intermediate Fold Logger

The next RHI architecture should begin passively, not interventionally.

For each branch and generated answer, log:

$$
\left\{
\ell,\ 
\tau_\ell,\ 
S_\ell,\ 
C_\ell,\ 
P_{\max,\ell},\ 
U_\ell
\right\}
$$

where:

- $\tau_\ell$ is the generated token at level $\ell$.
- $S_\ell$ is token or attention entropy.
- $C_\ell$ is attention concentration or proxy concentration.
- $P_{\max,\ell}$ is top-token confidence.
- $U_\ell$ is uncertainty/drift pressure.

### Token Entropy

If logits are available:

$$
S_\ell
=
-\sum_{v\in V}p_\ell(v)\log p_\ell(v).
$$

A normalized entropy ratio is:

$$
\hat{S}_\ell
=
\frac{S_\ell}{\log |V|}.
$$

### Top-Token Confidence

$$
P_{\max,\ell}
=
\max_{v\in V}p_\ell(v).
$$

### Concentration

A simple concentration proxy is:

$$
C_\ell
=
1-\hat{S}_\ell.
$$

If attention weights are available:

$$
C_\ell^{(\text{att})}
=
(\ell-1)\max_{k<\ell}p_k^{(\ell)}.
$$

This measures whether the model is diffusely averaging prior context or folding strongly back to specific anchor states.

## 10. The Lucas-Mask-Style Selector

The paper’s algebraic fold sections use Lucas masks to describe structured ancestral sampling in fold systems. A careful bridge can be built to transformer inference.

The attention mechanism should not yet be called a literal Lucas mask. The stronger and safer statement is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{attention behaves as a Lucas-mask-style selector.}
}
$$

It selects prior states and recombines them:

$$
R_\ell
=
\sum_{k<\ell}
w_k^{(\ell)}
R_k
+
\text{new contribution}.
$$

Where $w_k^{(\ell)}$ are attention weights.

This is structurally analogous to the fold sampling law, but it requires instrumentation before identity can be claimed.

Thus the addendum locks this rule:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Lucas-mask analogy is admissible as structure, not yet as theorem.}
}
$$

## 12. True Consensus vs Rubric Echo

The current terminal consensus gate checks whether branches agree at the audit/payload level.

However:

$$
\text{audit agreement}
\neq
\text{operational agreement}.
$$

Branches can agree because they all learned to repeat the rubric.

Intermediate logging allows a stronger test.

Let $R_\ell^{(i)}$ and $R_\ell^{(j)}$ be intermediate states for two branches.

Define:

$$
D_\ell^{(i,j)}
=
\|R_\ell^{(i)}-R_\ell^{(j)}\|.
$$

If branches converge early:

$$
D_\ell^{(i,j)} \downarrow
\quad
\text{for } \ell < \frac{L}{2},
$$

then consensus may reflect shared operational structure.

If branches only converge at the surface text level late in generation, the consensus may be rubric echo.

Thus:

$$
\Psi_{\text{consensus-real}}
\neq
\Psi_{\text{consensus-rubric-echo}}.
$$

This gives the next major testable distinction inside RHI.
