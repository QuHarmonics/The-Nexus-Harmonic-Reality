## 2. Scoped Implementation

A scoped implementation is the execution of one abstract law inside a bounded operating window.

Let the full field be

$$
\mathcal F.
$$

A scope is a restriction operator

$$
\mathcal S : \mathcal F \to \mathcal F_{\mathrm{loc}}
$$

where $\mathcal F_{\mathrm{loc}}$ is the local or bounded field visible to the implementation.

Then the system does not solve the full universal problem directly. It solves the scoped problem

$$
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{loc}} = \mathcal L \big|_{\mathcal F_{\mathrm{loc}}}.
$$

This yields the core definition:

$$
\boxed{
\text{scoped implementation}=
\text{local execution of a general law under bounded carrier and bounded readout}.
}
$$

The key parameters of scope are:

$$
\text{scope} = (\text{carrier},\ \text{resolution},\ \text{boundary},\ \text{tolerance},\ \text{address depth}).
$$

Change the scope, and the implementation looks different.  
But the abstract verb remains the same.

## 4. Compression and Expansion Are Directional Reads

Once a scope anchor is chosen, the same transform may appear either as compression or as expansion depending on the direction of reading.

Let the scoped projection be

$$
\Pi_\lambda : \mathcal F \to \mathcal R_\lambda
$$

where $\mathcal R_\lambda$ is the residue visible from scope anchor $\lambda$.

Then:

### Forward read — compression

From field to scoped residue:

$$
X \xrightarrow{\Pi_\lambda} \Pi_\lambda(X).
$$

This reads as compression because a larger possibility space is reduced to a local representable residue.

### Backward read — expansion

From scoped residue to constrained reconstruction:

$$
\Pi_\lambda(X) \xrightarrow{\mathcal D_\lambda} \hat X.
$$

This reads as expansion because the local residue is unfolded back into a larger or more structured field fragment.

So the exact inversion is:

$$
\boxed{
\text{compression and expansion are not opposites;}
\quad
\text{they are directional readings of the same scoped mapping.}
}
$$

## 8. Scope, Locality, and Address Depth

Scope is not merely smaller size. It is the determination of what can be locally resolved.

Let

$$
\mathcal A_\lambda
$$

be the addressable neighborhood around a scope anchor $\lambda$.

Then address depth can be formalized as

$$
d_\lambda = \dim(\mathcal A_\lambda)
$$

or, more generally, as the effective resolution depth needed to reconstruct a lawful local state.

A system with shallow address depth may compress strongly but reconstruct only coarsely.

A system with deep address depth may reconstruct richly but require more parity, more transport, and more repair.

Thus scope is coupled to address depth by:

$$
\boxed{
\text{scope} \leftrightarrow \text{what is locally recoverable}.
}
$$

This is why location matters so much. Location is not passive position. It is the evaluation site that determines what counts as nearby, relevant, recoverable, or compressible.

$$
\boxed{
