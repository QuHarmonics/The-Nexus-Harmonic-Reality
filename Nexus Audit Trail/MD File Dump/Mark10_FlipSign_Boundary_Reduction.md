## 2. The Closed Floor

### 2.1 Shape channel of a subtype

For a prime pair $(p,p+k)$ at wheel depth $W$, with admissible residue $r \in S_W(k)$ and midpoint

$$
H = p + \frac{k}{2},
$$

the shape channel is

$$
\boxed{
H \equiv r + \frac{k}{2} \pmod W.
}
$$

This is not a descriptive coordinate. It is the runtime constraint under which the pair is lawful.

### 2.2 Step Theorem

If $H_j$ and $H_{j+1}$ are consecutive midpoint centers in the same subtype, then

$$
\Delta H = H_{j+1}-H_j \equiv 0 \pmod W.
$$

The proof is immediate:

$$
H_{j+1}-H_j
=
\left(p_{j+1}+\frac{k}{2}\right)
-
\left(p_j+\frac{k}{2}\right)
=
p_{j+1}-p_j,
$$

and because both primes belong to the same subtype,

$$
p_{j+1}\equiv p_j\equiv r\pmod W,
$$

so

$$
p_{j+1}-p_j \equiv 0 \pmod W.
$$

Thus

$$
\boxed{
\Delta H \equiv 0 \pmod W.
}
$$

### 2.3 CRT propagation

If the theorem holds at wheel depth $W_m$ and the next prime is $q \nmid W_m$, then at

$$
W' = W_m q
$$

the subtype residue is fixed simultaneously modulo $W_m$ and modulo $q$. Since

$$
\Delta H \equiv 0 \pmod{W_m}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Delta H \equiv 0 \pmod q,
$$

the Chinese Remainder Theorem gives

$$
\Delta H \equiv 0 \pmod{W_m q}.
$$

So the Step Theorem propagates upward through the entire primorial tower.

## 5. Factor-Content Pressure

Define the odd-prime support of the gap:

$$
S(k)=\{\,q>3 : q\mid k\,\}.
$$

Now define the scalar pressure

$$
\boxed{
\Lambda(k)
=
\sum_{\substack{q\mid k\\ q>3}}
\log\!\left(\frac{q-1}{q-2}\right).
}
$$

This is just the logarithmic form of the Hardy–Littlewood multiplicative enhancement for the odd-prime support of $k$.

Equivalently, if

$$
C(k)
=
2C_2
\prod_{\substack{q\mid k\\ q>2}}
\frac{q-1}{q-2},
$$

then

$$
\log C(k)
=
\log(2C_2)
+
\Lambda(k).
$$

So $\Lambda(k)$ is the support-dependent part of the Hardy–Littlewood correction written additively.

### 5.1 Local pressure change

Now define

$$
\boxed{
\Delta\Lambda(k)
=
\Lambda(k)-\Lambda(k-6).
}
$$

Then:

- if the odd-prime support does not change from $k-6$ to $k$, we have
  $$
  \Delta\Lambda(k)=0,
  $$
- if a new odd prime enters or leaves the support, then
  $$
  \Delta\Lambda(k)\neq 0.
  $$

So:

$$
\boxed{
\Delta\Lambda(k)=0
\iff
S(k)=S(k-6),
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\Delta\Lambda(k)\neq 0
\iff
\text{the odd-prime support changes across the 6-step.}
}
$$

This is the first exact boundary detector.

## 7. Hot/Cold Frontier Law

The earlier nibble-runtime analysis introduced local heat as the logarithm of the admissible frontier size:

$$
H_j = \log_2 |A_j|.
$$

That gave the exact runtime meanings:

$$
|A_j|=1
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{cold},
$$

$$
|A_j|>1
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{hot}.
$$

The same grammar now transfers to prime-gap flip structure.

### 7.1 Cold rails

If

$$
\Delta\Lambda(k)=0,
$$

then the odd-prime support has not changed. The frame stays on the same arithmetic rail. These are **cold runs**.

### 7.2 Hot hinges

If

$$
\Delta\Lambda(k)\neq 0,
$$

then the gap crosses a support boundary and receives a new arithmetic kick. These are **hot hinges**.

So the flip problem becomes:

$$
\boxed{
\text{cold rail} \iff \Delta\Lambda(k)=0,
\qquad
\text{hot hinge} \iff \Delta\Lambda(k)\neq 0.
}
$$

This is the exact analog of the nibble frontier picture:

- constant support = unique continuation rail,
- support change = unresolved boundary with branching pressure.

## 8. Reduced Bias Law

At first order, the $W=6$ bias can be modeled as

$$
\boxed{
\mathrm{Bias}(x;k)
\approx
\Omega(x)+\beta\,\Lambda(k),
}
$$

where

- $\Omega(x)$ is the single oscillatory frame mode at sieve depth $x$,
- $\Lambda(k)$ is the odd-prime support pressure,
- $\beta>0$ is a scale factor.

Then a sign flip between $k-6$ and $k$ requires

$$
\operatorname{sgn}\!\bigl(\Omega(x)+\beta\Lambda(k-6)\bigr)
\neq
\operatorname{sgn}\!\bigl(\Omega(x)+\beta\Lambda(k)\bigr).
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{
\Delta\Lambda(k)\neq 0
\text{ is a necessary hinge condition for a new sign-flip driver.}
}
$$

Not every hot hinge must flip — the oscillatory mode may still stay on the same side of zero — but no new arithmetic flip driver exists away from a hinge.

This is the first genuine reduction of the flip problem.
