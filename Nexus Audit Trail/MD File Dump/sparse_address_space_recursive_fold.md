## 15. The Address-Rigidity Problem

The immediate theorem target is the same across all branches.

For a target observable $X$, let the predicted value from address $r$ be:

$$
\widehat{X}(r).
$$

Define the relative residual:

$$
\delta_X(r)
=
\frac{|\widehat{X}(r)-X_{\text{obs}}|}{|X_{\text{obs}}|}.
$$

Define the admissible set at tolerance $\tau$:

$$
A_X(\tau)=\{r:\delta_X(r)\le\tau\}.
$$

Then the sparsity score is:

$$
S_X(\tau)=|A_X(\tau)|.
$$

Small $S_X(\tau)$ means a sparse map.  
Large $S_X(\tau)$ means a dense decorative map.

A local uniqueness gap is:

$$
U_X=\delta_X(r_2)-\delta_X(r_1),
$$

where $r_1$ is the best-fit address and $r_2$ is the second-best.

Large $U_X$ means an isolated minimum.  
Small $U_X$ means a crowded basin.

This is the correct quantitative object for the next compile.
