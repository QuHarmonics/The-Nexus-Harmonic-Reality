## **2. Axiomatic Definition of PSREQ Cycles: Components and Formalism**

The axiomatic definition of PSREQ cycles as a tuple (P, S, E, Q) provides a structured approach to characterizing complex iterative systems. Each component plays a distinct and crucial role in defining the system\'s initiation, evolution, dynamics, and ultimate convergence criteria.

### **Detailed Breakdown of Each Component**

#### **P: Initial Seed Vector**

The component \'P\' is defined as an initial seed vector. This element represents the starting state or initial conditions from which the iterative process of a PSREQ cycle commences. Unlike many classical number theoretic problems that begin with a single scalar integer, such as the Collatz Conjecture which takes a positive integer \'n\', the designation \"vector\" implies a multi-dimensional input. This allows for a significantly richer initial configuration, potentially encompassing multiple interrelated parameters or encoding various properties of a single number (e.g., its prime factorization, binary representation, or related numbers like n+1 as explored in certain Collatz variants).

Mathematically, \'P\' could be an element of Zk, Rk, or a more abstract vector space, with the specific domain depending on the mathematical context of the system being modeled. For problems rooted in number theory, integer or rational vectors would be the most pertinent choices. The role of \'P\' is to precisely define the specific starting point from which the subsequent iterative transformations unfold. This generalization of initial conditions is a notable aspect of the PSREQ framework. In dynamical systems, initial conditions are paramount, and a vector representation enables the modeling of systems with multiple interacting variables or states. This broader scope could facilitate the simultaneous study of relationships between different Collatz-like sequences or states, offering a powerful tool for exploring higher-dimensional analogues of intractable number theoretic problems.

#### **S: Feedback Operator on Deviation**

The component \'S\' is conceptualized as a feedback operator acting on deviation. This signifies a transformation that is applied based on how the current state of the system deviates from a predetermined baseline, target, or specific characteristic. The term \"feedback\" inherently implies a closed-loop system where the output or current state directly influences the subsequent application of the operator. The notion of \"deviation\" suggests a metric or comparison that triggers specific actions.

Mathematically, \'S\' would represent a function or operator, S:X→Y, where X is the space of deviations and Y is the space of adjustments or transformations. For instance, in a simplified linear system, S(δ)=αδ+β. In the context of the Collatz Conjecture, the \"deviation\" could be interpreted as the parity of the current number (even or odd), and the corresponding operations (n/2 or 3n+1) are applied conditionally based on this deviation. This component directly captures the conditional and often non-linear nature of many iterative processes. The Collatz function itself is characterized as \"discontinuous, nonlinear, and chaotic\", and the abstract definition of \'S\' within the PSREQ framework is well-suited to model such complexities. In control theory and complex systems, feedback loops are fundamental to system behavior. The \"deviation\" aspect could quantify the distance from a target state (e.g., convergence to 1 in Collatz) or a specific property (e.g., parity, primality). This component is essential for modeling the \"unpredictable behavior\" frequently observed in systems like the Collatz sequence.

#### **E: Expansion Matrix via Recursion**

The component \'E\' is defined as an expansion matrix applied via recursion. This suggests a transformation designed to increase the \"size,\" \"magnitude,\" or \"complexity\" of the system\'s state. The term \"matrix\" typically implies linear transformations, but its application \"via recursion\" indicates iterative application, potentially with the matrix itself or its parameters being state-dependent (Ek​(xk​)).

The role of \'E\' is to model the growth or branching behavior inherent within the system\'s evolution. In the Collatz sequence, the 3n+1 operation is known to cause significant \"spikes\" in trajectory, with numbers soaring to unexpectedly high values before eventually decreasing, as exemplified by the sequence for n=27 reaching 9,232. This characteristic growth can be effectively modeled as an expansion. While 3n+1 is a scalar operation, an expansion matrix \'E\' could model how different components of the initial \"seed vector\" \'P\' grow or interact during these expansive phases. The recursive nature of \'E\' means it is applied repeatedly, capturing the multiplicative aspects of operations like 3n+1 (or 6n+2 in alternative formulations of Collatz) and their cumulative effect on the overall system state. This component is crucial for analyzing the \"highest point of trajectory\" or \"path records\" observed in computational verifications of the Collatz Conjecture, as well as the growth rates in other complex dynamical systems. The interplay between \'S\' (feedback/reduction) and \'E\' (expansion) is fundamental to understanding the overall trajectory and long-term behavior of a PSREQ system.

#### **Q: Projection to H ≈ 0.35 Eigenspace**

The component \'Q\' is defined as a projection to an H ≈ 0.35 eigenspace. This represents a linear transformation that maps the system\'s state onto a specific subspace, an \"eigenspace,\" which is characterized by a particular eigenvalue or property denoted by H ≈ 0.35. A \"projection\" implies a reduction in dimensionality or a focus on a specific, invariant characteristic of the system. An \"eigenspace\" suggests a stable or characteristic mode of behavior that the system eventually settles into or exhibits. The value H ≈ 0.35 is highly specific and warrants thorough examination.

The role of \'Q\' is to identify or isolate a critical characteristic of the system\'s long-term behavior, particularly its \"cyclical\" nature. The numerical value H ≈ 0.35 immediately invites comparison with the Riemann Hypothesis, which concerns the distribution of the non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function. The Riemann Hypothesis states that all non-trivial zeros lie on the \"critical line\" where the real part of the complex variable \'s\' is 1/2 (or 0.5). Extensive computational verification has confirmed that the first 10 trillion zeros of the zeta function indeed lie on this critical line. Furthermore, mathematical proofs have established that a significant percentage of *all* non-trivial zeros, specifically more than 41% (κ ≥ 0.4105), are located on this critical line.

A critical discrepancy arises when comparing the proposed H ≈ 0.35 with the established values. The value 0.35 does not directly correspond to the critical line\'s real part (0.5) nor to the proven percentage of zeros (0.4105). Several interpretations for this specific value can be considered. It is unlikely to be a simple misstatement or typo in an axiomatic definition. One possibility is that H ≈ 0.35 refers to a novel critical value associated with a different function, a modified Riemann zeta function, or a parameter within a mollifier function used in the study of zeta function zeros. Research on the Riemann Hypothesis often employs mollifiers (e.g., ψ1(s) and ψ2(s)) that involve various constants and parameters (e.g., θ1, θ2, R). While 0.35 is below the proven percentage of zeros, it could represent an optimized parameter in a specific mollifier construction that, when optimized, leads to certain analytical bounds or properties related to the critical line.

Another interpretation is that H ≈ 0.35 might refer to an alternative eigenspace related to the Riemann zeta function\'s properties but not directly to its zeros on the critical line. For instance, the Riemann Hypothesis is intrinsically linked to the distribution of prime numbers; it describes the deviation from the average distribution predicted by the prime number theorem. Thus, 0.35 could potentially represent a specific deviation threshold, a parameter in a different statistical model of primes, or a characteristic value in a related number-theoretic construct. The concept of projection onto an eigenspace is prevalent in linear algebra and quantum mechanics, as seen in theories like Yang-Mills, which involve field strength forms and generators of Lie algebra.^1^ This implies that the system, after undergoing various transformations, converges to a state where a particular characteristic, represented by this eigenspace, becomes dominant or invariant. This convergence to a stable, projected state forms the crucial link to the \"cycle\" aspect of the PSREQ definition.

### **Formalization of the \"Cycle\" Aspect**

Within the PSREQ framework, a \"cycle\" transcends the simple notion of a sequence that repeats. Instead, a PSREQ cycle is formally defined as a system where the iterative application of the feedback operator \'S\' and the expansion matrix \'E\', originating from an initial seed vector \'P\', eventually leads to a state that, when subjected to the projection \'Q\', falls into the H ≈ 0.35 eigenspace. Crucially, for all subsequent states within this established cycle, their projections via \'Q\' must either remain within or consistently return to this specific eigenspace.

This definition implies a profound stability or convergence criterion that is not merely observed but axiomatically imposed by the projection \'Q\'. For the Collatz Conjecture, the well-known trivial cycle is 1 → 4 → 2 → 1. In the PSREQ paradigm, this would mean that after a finite number of iterations, the state xk​ is such that Q(xk​) is an element of the H-eigenspace, and for all subsequent states xj​ within the cycle, Q(xj​) also consistently resides within this eigenspace. This axiomatic approach provides a powerful framework for defining and analyzing the stability and invariant properties of complex iterative systems.

## **3. Potential Application to the Collatz Conjecture**

The Collatz Conjecture, often described as \"the simplest unsolved problem in mathematics,\" involves a deceptively simple iterative process: if a number n is even, divide it by 2; if n is odd, multiply it by 3 and add 1. The conjecture states that this sequence always eventually reaches 1, regardless of the positive integer chosen as the starting value. Despite its straightforward rules, the behavior of Collatz sequences is highly unpredictable, exhibiting \"wild\" paths that can \"spike so high\" before converging to 1. This inherent unpredictability, coupled with its iterative nature, makes the Collatz Conjecture an ideal candidate for analysis under the PSREQ framework.

### **Modeling Collatz Dynamics with PSREQ Components**

The components of the PSREQ tuple offer a robust conceptual mapping for the Collatz process:

- **P (Initial Seed Vector):** For the Collatz Conjecture, the initial seed vector \'P\' would typically be a single positive integer n0​. However, the vector nature of \'P\' could be leveraged to encode additional properties of n0​, such as its parity, its prime factorization, or even a pair (n0​,n0​+1) as explored in some optimization algorithms for Collatz verification. This multi-component \'P\' could facilitate the study of relationships between different starting numbers or the impact of initial structural properties on trajectory.

- **S (Feedback Operator on Deviation):** This component directly models the conditional rules of the Collatz function. The \"deviation\" can be precisely defined as the parity of the current number nk​.

  - If nk​≡0(mod2) (even), the deviation triggers the operation nk​→nk​/2.

  - If nk​≡1(mod2) (odd), the deviation triggers the operation nk​→3nk​+1.\
    The Collatz function is a prime example of a discontinuous and non-linear system, which aligns well with the abstract nature of \'S\' as a feedback operator on deviation. This feedback mechanism is what drives the sequence\'s progression.

- **E (Expansion Matrix via Recursion):** The \'E\' component captures the growth phase of the Collatz sequence, particularly the 3n+1 operation. While 3n+1 is a scalar operation, an \'E\' matrix could be constructed to represent the transformation of a multi-component state vector that includes nk​ and potentially other derived properties. For instance, if \'P\' encodes (n0​,parity(n0​)), \'E\' could model how the 3n+1 operation affects both the magnitude and subsequent parity. The \"recursion\" aspect reflects the repeated application of these rules. The \"spikes\" in Collatz trajectories, where numbers temporarily \"soar up\", are direct manifestations of this expansion. Analyzing the properties of \'E\' could provide insights into the maximum values reached in Collatz sequences, known as \"path records\", which are crucial for computational verification efforts.

- **Q (Projection to H ≈ 0.35 Eigenspace):** This is the most abstract component in the Collatz context. If the Collatz Conjecture holds, every sequence eventually reaches the trivial cycle 1 → 4 → 2 → 1. In the PSREQ framework, this convergence to the 1-4-2-1 cycle would correspond to the system\'s state, after sufficient iterations, projecting into the H ≈ 0.35 eigenspace. The nature of this eigenspace for Collatz would need to be rigorously defined. It could represent a subspace where a specific characteristic of numbers in the 1-4-2-1 cycle (e.g., their binary structure, their behavior modulo a certain integer) becomes invariant under the Collatz operations. The challenge lies in defining a \'Q\' that captures this specific cyclical convergence for Collatz, and identifying what \'H ≈ 0.35\' would signify in this context. It might relate to a statistical property of the numbers within the cycle, or a measure of their \"distance\" from a state of convergence.

### **Insights into Collatz Behavior through PSREQ**

Applying the PSREQ framework to the Collatz Conjecture offers several avenues for deeper understanding. The explicit decomposition into P, S, E, and Q encourages a more structured analysis of the conjecture\'s sub-problems.

One significant aspect is the potential to analyze the interplay between the \"expansion\" (E) and \"reduction\" (S) phases. The Collatz conjecture\'s difficulty stems from the unpredictable balance between the 3n+1 (expansion) and n/2 (reduction) operations. The PSREQ framework compels a formal definition of these dynamics. For instance, the average number of steps to reach 1 is approximately log(n0​)/log(2/3​). This logarithmic relationship suggests a statistical balance between expansion and contraction. The PSREQ model could potentially formalize this balance by analyzing the eigenvalues of the combined S and E operations, or by examining the properties of the projected eigenspace.

Another area is the search for counterexamples. A counterexample to Collatz would be either an infinite divergent trajectory or a cycle different from 1-4-2-1.^2^ The PSREQ definition of a cycle, requiring projection into a specific eigenspace, provides a formal criterion for identifying such non-trivial cycles or divergence. If a sequence never projects into the defined H ≈ 0.35 eigenspace, it would either diverge or enter a different, unprojectable cycle. This formalization could guide computational searches, which have verified the conjecture up to

271, by providing a more abstract target for analysis beyond mere numerical values. The distributed computing efforts that verify Collatz convergence could potentially be informed by such a theoretical framework, guiding the search for specific types of trajectories or deviations.

## **4. Critical Examination of H ≈ 0.35 in the Context of the Riemann Hypothesis**

The inclusion of \"Q: projection to H ≈ 0.35 eigenspace\" within the axiomatic definition of PSREQ cycles necessitates a thorough examination, particularly in light of its implied connection to the Riemann Hypothesis. The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is widely regarded as the most important unsolved problem in pure mathematics.^3^ It asserts that all non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function

ζ(s) lie on the \"critical line\" where the real part of the complex variable s is 1/2.

### **Discrepancy and Interpretations of H ≈ 0.35**

The numerical value H ≈ 0.35 presents a significant point of divergence from established facts concerning the Riemann Hypothesis. The critical line is precisely Re(s) = 0.5. Furthermore, while the RH remains unproven, substantial progress has been made in computationally verifying the location of its zeros and proving that a positive percentage of them lie on the critical line. Specifically, the first 10 trillion non-trivial zeros have been computationally verified to lie on the critical line. More importantly, it has been mathematically proven that over 41% (specifically, κ≥0.4105) of *all* non-trivial zeros of the zeta function are located on this critical line.

The value H ≈ 0.35 is notably lower than both the critical line\'s real part (0.5) and the proven percentage of zeros (0.4105). This discrepancy suggests several possible interpretations for the user\'s proposed value:

1.  **Misinterpretation or Typographical Error:** While possible, it is generally improbable for an \"axiomatic definition\" to contain such a fundamental numerical error. Axiomatic definitions are intended to be precise.

2.  **Novel Critical Value for a Different Function:** H ≈ 0.35 might refer to a critical value for a mathematical function or system distinct from the classical Riemann zeta function, yet conceptually related to number theory or dynamical systems. The Riemann Hypothesis has numerous generalizations, such as the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions or the Grand Riemann Hypothesis for automorphic zeta functions, where non-trivial zeros are also expected to have a real part of 1/2.^3^ If H ≈ 0.35 pertains to a new or modified function, its precise definition and context would be crucial.

3.  **Parameter within a Mollifier Function:** Research aimed at proving a positive percentage of zeros on the critical line often employs \"mollifier\" functions. These are carefully constructed polynomials or Dirichlet series (e.g., ψ1​(s), ψ2​(s)) used to analyze the mean square of the zeta function and its derivatives. These mollifiers involve various parameters, such as θ1​ and θ2​ (where 0\<θ1​\<4/7) or R (a bounded positive real number). It is conceivable that H ≈ 0.35 could represent a specific value for one of these internal parameters, or a result derived from a particular configuration of such parameters within a specialized mollifier construction. However, it would not represent the real part of the zeros themselves or the overall percentage.

4.  **Alternative Eigenspace in a Related Mathematical Construct:** The \"eigenspace\" aspect of \'Q\' is significant. It implies that H ≈ 0.35 is an eigenvalue or a characteristic value of a projection operator that maps to a specific subspace. This subspace might describe properties of prime numbers or other number-theoretic objects that are *influenced* by the Riemann Hypothesis but are not directly the zeros of the zeta function itself. For instance, the Riemann Hypothesis controls the \"oscillations of primes around their \'expected\' positions\".^3^ H ≈ 0.35 could be a parameter related to such oscillations, a measure of deviation, or a threshold in a statistical model concerning prime distribution.

### **Implications for Number Theory and Prime Distribution**

If H ≈ 0.35 represents a meaningful parameter within the context of the Riemann Hypothesis or related number theory, its precise definition within the PSREQ framework would be paramount. The Riemann Hypothesis is deeply connected to the distribution of prime numbers. The existence of such an eigenspace, defined by H ≈ 0.35, could imply a new form of regularity or critical behavior in the seemingly chaotic distribution of primes. This would require a clear mathematical justification for the choice of 0.35 and a demonstration of its relevance to known or conjectured properties of primes.

The challenge lies in rigorously defining the operators P, S, E, and Q such that their iterative application and subsequent projection yield meaningful insights into the complex landscape of prime numbers or the Riemann zeta function.
