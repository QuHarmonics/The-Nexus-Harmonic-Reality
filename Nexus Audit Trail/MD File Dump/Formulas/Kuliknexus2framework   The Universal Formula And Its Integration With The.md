## 2. FOUNDATIONS OF THE NEXUS 2 FRAMEWORK

### 2.1. Mark 1 Harmonic Resonance
At the heart of Mark 1 is a ratio describing how potential and actualized energies relate across distinct subsystems:

\[
H_{\text{Mark1}} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} P_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} A_i}.
\]

- \( P_i \): Potential energy of subsystem \( i \).
- \( A_i \): Actualized (or realized) energy of subsystem \( i \).

This ratio is dimensionless and, in many contexts, aims to hover near a harmonic constant (e.g., 0.35). The harmonic resonance concept posits that many natural processes converge to or oscillate around a balance point, ensuring internal stability.


### 2.2. Samson’s Law (Feedback Stabilization)
Samson’s Law describes how systems self-correct by linking energy substitutions (\( \Delta E \)) over a timescale \( T \) to changes in force or input (\( \Delta F \)):

\[
S = \frac{\Delta E}{T}, \quad \Delta E = k \cdot \Delta F.
\]

- \( k \): Feedback constant, typically small (0.1 by default).
- \( T \): Time interval over which the energy shift occurs.

By including higher-order terms or multi-dimensional expansions, Samson’s Law can stabilize chaotic or rapidly varying conditions (e.g., via second-order derivatives or multi-dimensional partitioning). The fundamental premise is that timely feedback counters runaway imbalances.


### 2.3. Kulik Recursive Reflection (KRR)
Kulik Recursive Reflection characterizes how states progress or “reflect” over time:

\[
R(t) = R_0 \cdot e^{(H \cdot F \cdot t)},
\]

where \( R_0 \) is an initial reference, \( H \) is the harmonic constant, and \( F \) captures an external force or stimulus. In practice, KRR can be expanded to include branching or multi-dimensional factors for more complex systems. The key notion is that iterative exponentiation captures the growth (or decay) of a system’s reflective state, bridging micro-level patterns and macro-level expansions.
