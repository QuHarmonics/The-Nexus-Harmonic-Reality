2.1 Triangle as Topological Bridge
A degenerate triangle (points $A$, $B$, $C$ collinear) can be visualized as a straight rod or “bridge”
spanning between two endpoints. In our case, $A$–$C$ spans length 4, with $B$ sitting at 3 units from
$A$ (and 1 unit from $C$). This creates a π-ray configuration – the angle at $A$ is 180° (π radians, a
straight line ray), while at $B$ and $C$ the angles collapse to 0°. The usual notion of an internal area
bounded by the triangle is nullified. However, the structure still has measurable length ratios and
medians. Rather than discard the degenerate triangle as “ill-defined,” Nexus theory treats it as a
limiting case of a fold: the triangle has folded completely flat. In doing so, it becomes a bridge
connecting two segments (3 and 1) perfectly end-to-end. Topologically, it’s as if the triangle’s interior
was a loop that has unzipped into a line, creating a direct path or conduit. We call this a recursive Δ-fold
because one can imagine “unfolding” any triangular loop into a straight segment in iterative fashion –
here the recursion has reached its fixed point (full collapse). The degenerate triangle thus serves as a
boundary case between a 2D area and a 1D line – a boundary rich in meaning under Nexus
interpretation.
