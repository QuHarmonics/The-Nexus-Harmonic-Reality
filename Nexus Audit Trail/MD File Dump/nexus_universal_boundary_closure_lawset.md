## 4. What the Three Operators Mean

### 4.1 Binding

Binding is the persistence condition.

A local implementation must retain enough coherence to count as itself through a nonzero interval of change.

Formally, if $K_S \subseteq X_S$ is a coherence basin, then persistence means:

$$
\mathcal B_S(K_S) \subseteq K_S.
$$

More generally, over time-indexed evolution:

$$
\forall t \in [t_0,t_1],\qquad \mathcal B_{S,t}(K_S) \subseteq K_S.
$$

Binding is therefore not mere stasis. It is bounded self-coherence under ongoing process.

### 4.2 Transformation

Transformation is the becoming condition.

A local implementation must not be a frozen null. It must admit admissible change.

So:

$$
\exists x \in X_S \text{ such that } \mathcal T_S(x) \neq x.
$$

This is the minimum condition for history, unfolding, motion, metabolism, causality, or recursion.

Transformation is not noise. It is lawful state change.

### 4.3 Readout

Readout is the legibility condition.

A local implementation must expose difference across $\Gamma_S$.

Thus there must exist distinct states whose difference becomes readable:

$$
\exists x_1 \neq x_2 \in X_S
\quad\text{such that}\quad
\mathcal R_S(x_1) \neq \mathcal R_S(x_2).
$$

Readout is not secondary decoration. It is what makes relation, signaling, measurement, and communication possible.
