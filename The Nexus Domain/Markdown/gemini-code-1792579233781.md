## How the Map is Kept

Every entry is placed at the width `LEN` where it first holds, classified strictly by the job it performs there rather than its label. Whatever performs the ring's operation at a given width *is* that operation at that width, and the known mathematical or physical structure it meets there is the same path read at another location. An entry enters the map only when it **STANDS** or **RUNS**. A claim that ran and landed elsewhere remains on the map as a **DEAD END** with its true owner named, and every correction is preserved.

**Notation**: A word $x$ has $n = \text{LEN}$ bits, bit $i$ is the coefficient of $2^i$, mirror pair $j$ is $\{n-1-j, j\}$, $h = \lfloor n/2 \rfloor$, and $P = 2^{\lceil n/2 \rceil}$ is the number of palindromes. $J$ reverses, $C$ complements, the partner is $P(x) = J(x)$ when $J(x) \neq x$ and $C(x)$ when $x$ is a palindrome, and $R_n$ lists representatives ascending ($0 \dots M-1$) with their partners facing them ($N-1-i$) and the centre seam pair ($M-1, M$) exchanged.

## Layer C. The Bit Stream and the Pinch Mechanism

Frame $s$ reads the $L$ bits starting $s$ bits into each word: $w_i^{(s)} = (R_L[i] \bmod 2^{L-s})\cdot 2^s + \lfloor R_L[i+1]/2^{L-s} \rfloor$.

* **Frame Law [RUNS §24, $L = 2\text{--}22$]**: With $h = \lfloor L/2 \rfloor$ and $P = 2^{\lceil L/2 \rceil}$, the loss (number of missing words) is $0$ for $s \le 1$; $P(1 - 2^{2-s})$ for $2 \le s \le h-1$ (rising branch); $P - 2$ (even $L$) or $P - 6$ (odd $L$) at $s = h$; $P - 2 - 2^{s-h+1}$ for $h+1 \le s \le L-2$ (falling branch); and $P - 2$ at $s = L-1$.
* **The Pinch Law Below the Middle ($2 \le s \le h-1$) [RUNS §27, $L = 6\text{--}22$, 81 cells]**:
  * Write each frame word as the left rotation $\operatorname{rotL}_s(V)$, where $V$ is the $s$-bit head of the next word followed by the $(L-s)$-bit tail of the current word.
  * On the representative face, $V$ is the word itself except at the end of each $s$-bit prefix block, where the head steps to the next block. On the partner face, between two non-palindromic partners, $V = J(G(a))$, where $G(a)$ keeps the top $L-s$ bits of representative $a$ and takes its low $s$ bits from the representative before it in its block $q(a)$.
  * A palindromic representative $p = \tau \parallel \beta$ (with top $L-s$ bits $\tau$, low $s$ bits $\beta$, and predecessor $q(p)$ with low $s$ bits $\beta'$) breaks this pattern at its partner $C(p)$: the exit window starting at $C(p)$ says $\operatorname{rotL}_s(J(C(\tau)\parallel \beta'))$ where the reversal pattern puts $P(q(p))$, and the entry window ending at $C(p)$ says $\operatorname{rotL}_s(J(\tau \parallel C(\beta)))$ where the pattern puts $p$.
  * Thus $p$ **pinches** two windows into words already said elsewhere, leaving $C(p)$ and $P(q(p))$ (rotated by $s$) unsaid. The pinch opens if and only if $\beta' = C(\beta)$, which holds exactly when the top $s-1$ bits of $p$ are all zero (outer $s-1$ mirror pairs are $00$).
* **Exact Collision Pairing Below the Middle [RUNS §27, 45,328 pairs]**:
  * With $z_1 = C(\tau)\parallel \beta'$ and $z_2 = \tau \parallel C(\beta)$, the exit window starting at $C(p)$ meets the window starting at $P(\operatorname{next}(z_1))$ when $z_1$ is a representative (partner face, $1/4$ of collisions) and the window starting at $J(z_1)$ when $z_1$ is not a representative (representative face, $1/4$ of collisions).
  * The entry window ending at $C(p)$ always meets the window starting at $P(\operatorname{next}(z_2))$ on the partner face ($2/4$ of collisions), because $z_2$ is always a representative in the block of $p$.

## Layer F. Structural Derivations for Round 2 (§28)

### 1. The Falling-Branch Pinch & Escape Decomposition ($h + 1 \le s \le L - 2$)
When the offset $s$ crosses the midpoint $h$ ($h + 1 \le s \le L - 2$), the remaining tail of the current word $R_L[i]$ has length $r = L - s \in \{2, \dots, \lceil L/2 \rceil - 1\}$ (shorter than a half-word), while the head of the next word $R_L[i+1]$ has length $s = L - r > h$ (longer than a half-word):
$$w_i^{(L-r)} = \operatorname{suffix}_r(R_L[i]) \parallel \operatorname{prefix}_{L-r}(R_L[i+1])$$
Because the long head of length $L - r > h$ crosses the centre of the word, staying inside the same $(L-r)$-bit head block while opening the pinch on the $r$-bit tail requires the canonical palindrome $p$ to have its outer $r - 1 = L - s - 1$ pairs equal to $00$ and its $r$-th bit equal to $1$ (`0^{L-s-1}1` on the canonical face, which complements to **`1^{L-s-1}0`** on the partner face).
* Fixing the first $L - s$ bits of a partner-face palindrome to $1^{L-s-1}0$ leaves $\lceil L/2 \rceil - (L - s) = s - \lfloor L/2 \rfloor = s - h$ free palindrome bits, yielding $N_{\text{pal-escape}}(L, s) = 2^{s - h}$ escaping palindromes.
* Each such palindrome escapes on both its entry and exit windows ($2 \cdot 2^{s-h} = 2^{s-h+1}$ windows), which together with the $2$ wrap/seam boundary escapes accounts for the exact falling-branch loss:
  $$\operatorname{loss}(L, s) = P - 2 - 2^{s - h + 1}$$

### 2. The Seam and Boundary Cells ($s = h$ and $s = L - 1$)
* **At $s = L - 1$ ($r = 1$)**: The falling-branch escape prefix $1^{r-1}0$ becomes $0$ (requiring a partner-face palindrome to start with $0$). Since every partner-face palindrome begins with $1$, zero interior palindromes escape ($N_{\text{pal-escape}} = 0$), leaving only the $2$ boundary escapes: $\operatorname{loss}(L, L-1) = P - 2$.
* **At $s = h$ (Midpoint)**: For even $L = 2h$, the centre seam swap exchanges the $h$-bit halves of $1^{h-1}01^h$ and $1^h01^{h-1}$ directly across the cut $s = h$, leaving $\operatorname{loss}(2h, h) = P - 2$. For odd $L = 2h + 1$, the free middle bit doubles the all-ones outer palindrome class and frees $4$ additional seam-pair windows, giving $\operatorname{loss}(2h+1, h) = P - 6$.

### 3. Proof of the Successor's 6 Optimal Orders ($n \ge 6$)
For $T(x) = R_n[(\operatorname{address}(x) + 1) \bmod 2^n]$:
1. No single arrival ($t = 1$) or pair of arrivals ($t = 2$) can decide any output bit of $T(x)$, because even the LSB $T(x)_0$ depends simultaneously on $x_0$, the face ($x_{n-1}$ vs $x_0$), and the next carry/borrow bit. Thus all $3! = 6$ permutations of the opening triple $\{0, 1, n-1\}$ have identical readiness at $t = 1, 2, 3$.
2. After $n-2$ closes pair 1 at $t = 4$, for every subsequent mirror pair $j \in \{2, \dots, h-1\}$, revealing the low bit $j$ before its mirror $n-1-j$ makes output bit $T(x)_j$ ready $1$ step earlier on all words whose face is already decided at depth $< j$ (since $x + 1$ on the representative face needs only the bottom-up carry $\{0, \dots, j\}$). This locks the unique tail $n-2, (2, n-3), (3, n-4), \dots$ and yields exactly $3! \times 1 = 6$ optimal orders.

### 4. The Two-Wheel Address Switch Point $k(n)$ and Back-Half Reversal
In $\operatorname{rank}(x) = Q(f) + D(f,g)$, front-half core bits ($b \ge h$) govern the row parabola $Q(f)$ in descending significance ($n-1-k \to h$), whereas back-half core bits ($b < h$) are ordered inside $D(f,g)$ by their **bit-reversal $J_h(g)$**, where significance runs bottom-up ($0 \to h-1$). This opposing significance in $J_h(g)$ explains why the last two back-half core bits transpose at $n = 8$ `(5, 4, 2, 3)`, $n = 9$ `(4, 5, 3)`, $n = 10$ `(6, 5, 3, 4)`, and $n = 17$ `[..., 5, 6]`.
