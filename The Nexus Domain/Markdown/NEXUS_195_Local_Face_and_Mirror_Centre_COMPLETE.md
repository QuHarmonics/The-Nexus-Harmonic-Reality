## 1. Derivation in one residue-address base

For a local hairpin with outgoing end `e` and returning start `s`, row `t` transposes the two residue addresses

$$ (l_t,r_t)=(e-t,s+t), \qquad l_t+r_t=e+s=S. $$

The exact pair-word rule with an `m`-bit address per residue is

$$W(i,j)=b_m(i)\,\Vert\,J(b_m(j)), \qquad J(W(i,j))=W(j,i).$$

This is **address transposition**, not a demand that the two residues have the same side-chain identity or chemical value. `t mod 2` sets the ideal alternating row and, in an ideal β sheet, the alternating presentation of side chains. The separate chemical-value bit specified for the sequence-only crease test is

$$v_i=0 \text{ for }i\in\{G,A,V,L,I,F,P\},\qquad v_i=1\text{ otherwise}.$$

The candidate **value-mirror** hypothesis additionally demands $v_i=v_{S-i}$, a stronger statement than address transposition. On an even `S`, the central self-cell is omitted from the outward matching score. The score is the number of consecutive successful comparisons from the centre, capped at 8. A score is computed at every potential `S` independently of structural labels.

The physical face sign cannot be inferred from address parity alone unless the sheet embedding is supplied. With the outgoing-strand tangent and the interstrand bridge taken from *known* coordinates, define the local oriented sheet normal

$$ \hat n_t = \frac{(\mathbf C\alpha_{l_t-1}-\mathbf C\alpha_{l_t+1})\times(\mathbf C\alpha_{r_t}-\mathbf C\alpha_{l_t})}{\left\|(\mathbf C\alpha_{l_t-1}-\mathbf C\alpha_{l_t+1})\times(\mathbf C\alpha_{r_t}-\mathbf C\alpha_{l_t})\right\|}, $$

and for any available Cβ

$$ f_i=\operatorname{sign}[(\mathbf C\beta_i-\mathbf C\alpha_i)\cdot\hat n_t]. $$

The two matched residues share a **local physical face** if their signs agree under that same local normal. This construction follows sheet twist; a single global planar normal need not. The physical sign label depends on the chosen orientation convention, but agreement and oppositeness do not. It is **not derived solely from the local N–CA–C–Cβ stereocentre**: it also requires the partner arm and adjacent Cα positions.

The side-chain stereocentre was separately checked with the oriented local tetrahedral volume

$$\chi_i=[(N_i-C\alpha_i)\times(C'_i-C\alpha_i)]\cdot(C\beta_i-C\alpha_i).$$

All 17,985 evaluable recorded non-Gly Cβ stereocentres had the same positive sign under this convention. Rigid motions preserve it, while a mirror reflection reverses it. This is a readback of local handedness, not a sufficient condition for an assembled antiparallel sheet.
