## Exact correction 1: the printed LEN-3 polynomial

Section 1.1 gives

$$P_3(a,b,c)=(1\oplus a\oplus c,\,1\oplus a\oplus b\oplus c,\,1\oplus a).$$

But applying the native definition $P_3(w)=J_3(w)\oplus s_3(w)111$ with $s_3(w)=1\oplus a\oplus c$ yields

$$\boxed{P_3(a,b,c)=(1\oplus a,\,1\oplus a\oplus b\oplus c,\,1\oplus c).}$$

The printed expression differs from the defined operator on six of eight inputs. For example, it fixes `001`, whereas native facing maps `001` to `100`. The corrected expression matches all eight inputs. The selected *operator* and its main theorem remain intact; the explicitly expanded polynomial must change.

## Exact correction 2: the uniqueness claim on palindromes

The sentence “the unique distinction on $\mathrm{Pal}_n$ that commutes with $J_n$ is the root complement $C_n$” is false if uniqueness is supposed to follow merely from commutation or fixed-point-free involution constraints. On $\mathrm{Pal}_n$, $J_n$ is the identity, so commutation with $J_n$ cannot select a unique partner.

At $n=3$, define $Q=J_3$ on nonpalindromes, while on palindromes let $Q(a,b,a)=(a,1\oplus b,a)$. Then $Q$ is a fixed-point-free involution, commutes with both $J_3$ and $C_3$, and yet $Q(000)=010\ne111=P_3(000)$.

**Precise replacement:** “On a palindrome, reversal supplies no distinction; selecting the root complement as the required fallback defines native facing.” This expresses the root-NOT interface contract explicitly rather than claiming an unsupported uniqueness theorem.

## Exact correction 3: group designation

The source calls $G_n\cong(\mathbb Z_2)^n\rtimes\mathbb Z_n$, of order $n2^n$, the “full hyperoctahedral wreath product.” The order and the semidirect-product description are right, but the designation is not: the full hyperoctahedral group is $(\mathbb Z_2)^n\rtimes S_n$ of order $n!2^n$.

**Precise replacement:** “the signed cyclic-shift group, equivalently the regular wreath product $\mathbb Z_2\wr\mathbb Z_n$, of order $n2^n$.” Independent enumeration at $n=2,3,4,5$ returned $8,24,64,160$, whereas the full hyperoctahedral orders are $8,48,384,3840$.
