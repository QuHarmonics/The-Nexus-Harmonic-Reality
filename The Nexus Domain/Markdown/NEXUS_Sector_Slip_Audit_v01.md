## Exact address algebra

- `D+ : (i,j)->(i+1,j+1)` preserves `S mod 2` and `d=j-i mod 2`.
- `D- : (i,j)->(i+1,j-1)` preserves `S mod 2` and `d mod 2`.
- `B_i : (i,j)->(i+1,j)` changes `S` by `+1` and `d` by `-1`.
- `B_j : (i,j)->(i,j+1)` changes `S` by `+1` and `d` by `+1`.

Thus a one-sided register step is a candidate **local** sector-switch operation. Witness: `(3,9)` with `S=12` → `B_i` → `(4,9)` with `S=13` → ordinary `D-` → `(5,8)` with `S=13`. This is a confirmed address-space transition; it is **not** a predicted or confirmed protein contact.

The native four-bit partner `P_4` changes pair-address parity on 8/16 words. **None** of these eight P-edges, under the native R2 word-to-address map used in the prior NEXUS tests, is a local single-coordinate increment/decrement. Therefore `P` is not automatically the physical beta-bulge step.

## Missing physical typing

The notebook's `AA_SLOT_MAP` was introduced as side-chain atom-slot profiles, but the compiler uses `open_11` and `open_00` as backbone H-N donor / C=O acceptor status. Those are different interfaces: alanine, valine, leucine and isoleucine have backbone amide donors unless blocked chemically (as at proline), even if their side chains have no donor. It also checks donor at earlier `i` and acceptor at later `j` for the helix case, whereas the standard `i → i+4` carbonyl-to-amide contact is **acceptor O_i → donor NH_(i+4)**. There are 69 sequence-eligible backbone `i -> i+4` pairs excluding later-proline N-H donors, versus 23 helix labels in this notebook. **69 is merely eligibility, not 69 observed helix hydrogen bonds.**

The sign rule `face(i)*face(i+1)>=0` is a test on side-chain composition, not a geometric test of inward/outward orientation or paired-strand hydrogen-bond registry. Moreover zero signatures satisfy it automatically. It cannot select `B_i` versus `B_j` from the declared information.
