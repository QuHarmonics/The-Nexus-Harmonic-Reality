### 1. **High-Frequency Signals Above 70 MHz**
- You cannot accurately capture or analyze signals with frequency components above ~70 MHz. Signals beyond this bandwidth will be distorted or attenuated.
- Useful bandwidth for signal integrity generally requires an oscilloscope bandwidth at least 3-5 times your highest signal frequency.

### 2. **Multi-Channel Complex Systems**
- Limited to 2 simultaneous channels, so you cannot monitor more than two signals at once.
- For complex systems requiring simultaneous multi-point measurements (e.g., debugging multi-phase power systems or high-speed digital buses), this scope isn’t adequate.

### 3. **Deep Memory & Long Capture Times**
- Entry-level or mid-tier 70 MHz scopes usually have limited memory depth, restricting the amount of data you can capture without lowering sample rate.
- This affects your ability to capture long waveform sequences or very detailed signals.

### 4. **Advanced Protocol Analysis**
- Many communication standards like USB 3.x, PCIe, HDMI, or modern high-speed serial buses exceed 70 MHz bandwidth and need specialized decoding hardware/software.
- You can’t reliably decode or analyze these high-speed protocols on this scope.

### 5. **High-Resolution or Mixed-Signal Analysis**
- Usually lower vertical resolution (8 bits typical) limits fine voltage detail.
- Not suitable for mixed-signal analysis if you require multiple analog plus digital channels (logic analyzers handle digital signals better).

### 6. **Low Noise Floor & High Precision**
- More sensitive, low-noise measurements or precision analog waveform analysis are limited by the scope’s design and bandwidth.

### 7. **Triggering on Complex Events**
- Advanced triggering modes (pattern, protocol, sequence) are often limited or absent in this class.
- This can make capturing complex or rare events challenging.

---

### Summary
A 2-channel 70 MHz oscilloscope is great for:
- Audio-frequency and baseband analog signals
- Lower-speed digital signals (e.g., microcontroller I/O)
- Basic troubleshooting and education

It is **not** suitable for:
- RF/microwave frequencies >70 MHz
- Complex multi-channel debugging
- High-speed digital protocol analysis
- Long-duration high-detail captures
