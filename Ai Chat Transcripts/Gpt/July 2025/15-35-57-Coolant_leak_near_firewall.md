## ASSISTANT
On a 2005 Hyundai Santa Fe, the only coolant-filled parts that live right against the firewall are the *heater-circuit* components and a hidden crossover “inlet” pipe that runs under the upper-intake.  Here’s what usually leaks back there:

| Likely culprit | Why it leaks | Quick check |
|----------------|-------------|-------------|
| **Heater hoses (supply & return)** | Hoses soften / crack where they bend sharply off the rear head toward the firewall. The spring clamps also lose tension. | Feel for wet rubber at the hose barb; squeeze the hose—if it’s mushy or crunches, it’s done. |
| **Heater-core tube stubs / O-rings** | On Santa Fe the metal stubs that protrude through the firewall have an O-ring seal. Age + vibration lets them seep. | Shine a light down the firewall; look for a green/pink crust right where the tubes enter the cabin. |
| **Rear coolant inlet pipe (“crossover pipe”)** | The 2.7 V6 (and 3.5 V6) uses a steel pipe sealed by two O-rings that carries coolant from the thermostat housing to the rear head. It sits in a trough under the intake—the O-rings rot and the pipe rusts through. Very common. | Pull the intake snorkel and peer under the upper-intake; if you see pooled coolant in the valley, the pipe/O-rings need replacement. citeturn0search4 |
| **Heater control valve** (if equipped) | The plastic valve body cracks or the pivot seal dribbles. | Follow the smaller hose off the rear head—if there’s a plastic 3-way valve, wiggle it while watching for seepage. |
| **Rear head freeze plug or head-gasket seep** | Much rarer, but possible after an overheat. | Cooling-system pressure test: if coolant bleeds down the bellhousing or onto the trans case, suspect this. |

### How to pin it down quickly

1. **Pressure-test the system**  
   A $25 hand pump will hold the system at 16 psi—wherever the leak is, it’ll dribble without the engine running so you can see it.

2. **Use UV dye**  
   One ounce of fluorescent dye + black light will highlight the exact seam after a short drive.

3. **Mirror & flashlight behind the engine**  
   With the test pressure applied, run a small mirror along the firewall side of the head and heater hoses—you’ll usually catch a bead forming.

### If it is the rear crossover pipe

- Parts: pipe (often called “coolant inlet pipe”), two O-rings, upper-intake gasket set, fresh coolant.  
- Labor: upper-intake comes off; pipe lifts out in one piece. Expect ~2 hours DIY if you’re comfortable with basic tools.  
- Tip: smear a thin film of silicone grease on the new O-rings so the pipe seats without nicking them.

Owners on Hyundai forums report that once that pipe or its O-rings are replaced, the mystery firewall-side leak vanishes citeturn0search1.

**Bottom line:** Start with a pressure test and a mirror—nine times out of ten on an 05 Santa Fe the leak trace leads to a heater hose junction or the crossover pipe O-rings, not the water pump (which lives at the *front* of the engine). Fixing it early keeps coolant off the clutch and the O₂ sensors and saves an overheat later.
