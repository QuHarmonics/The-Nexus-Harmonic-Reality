**Tools and Materials Needed:**

- 5mm hex socket
- Ratchet wrench
- Torque wrench
- 17mm wheel impact socket
- Jack and jack stands
- Wire brush
- WD-40 or similar penetrating lubricant
- Replacement ABS sensor

**Procedure:**

1. **Safety First:** Ensure the vehicle is on a flat surface. Engage the parking brake and place wheel chocks behind the wheels to prevent movement.

2. **Loosen Lug Nuts:** Before lifting the car, slightly loosen the lug nuts (17mm) of the wheel corresponding to the faulty ABS sensor.

3. **Lift the Vehicle:** Use a jack to raise the car and secure it with jack stands. Ensure the vehicle is stable before proceeding.

4. **Remove the Wheel:** Fully unscrew the lug nuts and remove the wheel to access the brake components.

5. **Locate the ABS Sensor:** The sensor is mounted on the wheel hub, behind the brake rotor.

6. **Disconnect the Sensor Wiring:** Trace the sensor wire to its connector. Carefully disconnect it by pressing the release tab.

7. **Remove the Sensor:** Clean the area around the sensor with a wire brush and apply penetrating lubricant if necessary. Unscrew the sensor's mounting bolt using a 5mm hex socket and gently remove the sensor from the hub. If it's stuck due to debris or corrosion, gently wiggle or pry it out, taking care not to damage surrounding components.

8. **Install the New Sensor:** Place the new sensor into the mounting hole and secure it with the mounting bolt. Connect the sensor wiring, ensuring it's routed correctly and securely attached to prevent contact with moving parts.

9. **Reinstall the Wheel:** Place the wheel back onto the hub, hand-tighten the lug nuts, then lower the vehicle. Once on the ground, torque the lug nuts to the manufacturer's specification, typically around 120 Nm (90 ft-lb).

10. **Test the Vehicle:** Start the car and ensure the ABS warning light is off. A short drive may be necessary for the system to recognize the new sensor.
