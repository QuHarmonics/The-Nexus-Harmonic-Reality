When selecting a pressure-tank capacity for a typical domestic water system (one serving standard appliances such as a dishwasher, clothes washer, shower and lavatory fixtures), the two chief considerations are:

1. **Pump short‐cycling and motor wear**  
   - Smaller tanks (e.g. 20 gal nominal) have relatively low *usable* draw-down.  After accounting for the cut-in/cut-out differential (typically 30–50 psi), a 20 gal bladder tank will only supply on the order of 6–8 gal of water before the pump must restart.  This can lead to very frequent pump cycles (often > 20 starts per hour), which significantly increases motor wear and electrical consumption.  
   - A 36 gal tank, by contrast, typically provides 12–15 gal of draw-down under the same pressure differential.  Fewer starts per hour both extends pump life and improves overall system efficiency.

2. **Pressure stability (user comfort and appliance protection)**  
   - With a smaller tank, users may experience noticeable pressure drop (e.g. from 50 psi down toward 30 psi) even during modest simultaneous demands (shower plus dishwasher).  
   - A larger tank cushions the pressure fluctuations, so that two or more fixtures can run concurrently with minimal perceptible dip in delivery pressure.

- **Example:** If your household uses 100 gal in a morning peak (two showers + laundry), a 20 gal tank would require roughly 15 pump cycles to keep pace, whereas a 36 gal tank would need only about 7 cycles.

### Recommendation

For a single-family residence with **“standard”** simultaneous uses, a **36 gal** pressure tank is almost universally preferred over a 20 gal unit, because it

- **Reduces pump cycling** (fewer but longer cycles),  
- **Improves pressure stability** during multi‐fixture use, and  
- **Extends pump and motor life**, lowering lifetime operating costs.

A 20 gal tank can suffice for very light use (e.g. a small cabin or point-of-use supply), but for full-house coverage—including dishwasher, washing machine and multiple bathrooms—the extra buffer of a 36 gal tank delivers a markedly better experience and greater equipment longevity.
