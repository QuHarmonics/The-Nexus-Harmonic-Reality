Varispeed—slowing the beat for tracking and then speeding it back up—is almost always handled in your DAW or on a console with built‑in DSP, not by the audio interface itself. Here’s how you’d typically pull it off with Pro Tools (or any DAW) and your new rack‑mount interface:

---

## 1. Use Pro Tools’ Varispeed Plugin (or Native Varispeed)

1. **Insert a Master Fader** in your session.  
2. **On that Master Fader**, insert the Avid **Varispeed** plugin (AudioSuite > Pitch Shift > Varispeed) or, if you’re on an HDX/TDM system, enable the Transport‑Varispeed function.  
3. **Dial the speed down** (e.g. –20 %) so the beat plays back slower and pitched down.  
4. **Record the vocal** while Shaggy (or Violent J!) raps over the slowed track.  
5. **Bypass/remove** the plugin (or disable Varispeed) and the session returns to normal tempo/pitch—your vocal track will now play back faster and pitched up, matching the original beat.  

> **Pros:** Instant, zero‑latency; you hear exactly what you’ll get.  
> **Cons:** Only available in Pro Tools (Native via AudioSuite, HDX/TDM via Transport).

---

## 2. Sample‑Rate Trick (Session at Lower SR)

1. **Create a new session** at, say, **38 kHz** instead of 44.1 kHz.  
2. **Import your beat**—it’ll play ~14 % slower and pitched down.  
3. **Record the vocal** at that rate.  
4. **Bounce or export** the vocal track.  
5. **Re‑import** into your original 44.1 kHz session. It’ll speed back up and pitch up to match the beat.  

> **Pros:** Works in any DAW; no plugin needed.  
> **Cons:** You have to manage two sessions and offline bounces; no real‑time control.

---

## 3. Third‑Party “Tape‑Stop” / Pitch‑Shift Plugins

If you’re not on Pro Tools HDX/TDM, you can grab a real‑time pitch/tempo plugin on your master bus:

- **Waves SoundShifter** (real‑time mode)  
- **Serato Pitch ’n Time**  
- **iZotope Vinyl** (free, tape‑stop preset)  

You simply slow the master by –X % in the plugin GUI, record, then automate it back to 0 % for playback.

---

### Bottom Line

- **No rack‑mount interface** (RME, MOTU, Antelope, etc.) has a built‑in varispeed control like Avid’s HDX/TDM consoles.  
- **Pick your interface** purely on preamp/conversion/drivers: RME Fireface UCX II, MOTU 828es, Antelope Orion Studio, etc., and slot it behind your 1073SPX → LA‑2A → 1176 chain.  
- **Handle varispeed** in Pro Tools (Varispeed plugin or Transport varispeed), via a lower‑SR session, or with a third‑party real‑time pitch plugin.

That way you keep your new \$1.7 K 1073SPX and \$1–2 K interface—and still get the exact “slow‑the‑beat, rap, speed it back up” trick ICP relies on.

### Why the Aurora can’t do varispeed

1. **No transport or varispeed DSP**  
   Unlike Avid HDX/TDM hardware (which hooks into Pro Tools’ transport for real‑time speed changes), the Aurora just digitizes whatever clock it’s fed. There’s no “slow down by –20%” button in the unit.  

2. **Fixed sample‑rate operation**  
   You set the Aurora to 44.1, 48, 88.2 kHz, etc., but you can’t smoothly ramp the clock rate up/down mid‑session to emulate tape speed changes.  

3. **Monitoring mixer is minimal**  
   Some Aurora models have a very basic cue‑mix section, but it doesn’t include pitch‑shift or varispeed algorithms.

---

### How to get that “slow the beat, rap, speed back up” workflow with an Aurora

You’ll need to handle the varispeed in your DAW (or via outboard gear) and still use the Aurora for conversion:

1. **DAW‑based Varispeed**

   - In other DAWs: insert a real‑time pitch/tempo plugin on the master bus (e.g. Waves SoundShifter, Serato Pitch ’n Time, iZotope Vinyl).  
   - **Workflow**: slow the master, track vocals, then bypass/unload the plugin to return to normal speed.

2. **Session sample‑rate trick**  
   - Create a temporary session at a lower sample rate (e.g. 38 kHz). Import the beat so it plays slower. Track vocals, then bounce and re‑import into your 44.1 kHz session so everything speeds back up.  

3. **Outboard tape or sampler**

---

## 1. Historical & Technical Roots

- **Tape‑machine heritage**  
  Varispeed originally comes from reel‑to‑reel decks—change the capstan speed and everything (pitch + tempo) shifts together. Avid built their HDX hardware and Sync HD box to emulate that “tape‑machine” workflow in the digital domain, tying varispeed directly into the hardware transport.  
- **DSP integration**  
  HDX cards carry AAX DSP cores that sit underneath Pro Tools’ transport engine. Varispeed isn’t just a plugin—it’s baked into the transport clock that the HDX/Sync HD hardware feeds to the DAW.  

---

## 2. Why Other Interfaces & DAWs Don’t

1. **Different priorities**  
   Manufacturers like RME, MOTU, Apogee, Antelope, UA, SSL, etc., focus their DSP on:  
   - Ultra‑low‑latency monitoring  
   - Onboard EQ/compression/reverb for zero‑latency tracking  
   - Clocking stability and conversion fidelity  
   Varispeed is a niche pro‑studio feature, and dedicating hardware resources (and driver complexity) to it hasn’t been a high‑priority.  

2. **Software solutions suffice for most users**  
   Every major DAW—Logic, Cubase, Live, Studio One, Reaper, even Pro Tools Standard—offers software‑based varispeed or time‑stretch plugins. Because these run on the host CPU (or as AudioSuite in Pro Tools), manufacturers haven’t felt the need to replicate it in their hardware.  

3. **Complexity vs. demand**  
   Implementing real‑time, glitch‑free varispeed at the hardware clock level requires tight integration between the interface’s FPGA/DSP, its driver, and the DAW transport. With relatively low demand outside high‑end post and scoring stages, it hasn’t paid off for most interface makers.  

---

## 3. “Competitors” in the Varispeed Space

- **DAW‑native varispeed**  
  - Pro Tools (AudioSuite Varispeed)  
  - Logic Pro’s Varispeed mode  
  - Cubase’s Real‑Time Time Stretch  
  - Ableton Live’s Warp  
- **Real‑time plugins**  
  - Waves SoundShifter  
  - Serato Pitch ’n Time  
  - iZotope Radius (in older versions)  
- **Outboard tape/sampler** (as we discussed): Tascam, Otari decks or Elektron/AKAI samplers  

No other manufacturer has built a **hardware transport‑level** varispeed button into their interface the way Avid has with HDX + Sync HD.
