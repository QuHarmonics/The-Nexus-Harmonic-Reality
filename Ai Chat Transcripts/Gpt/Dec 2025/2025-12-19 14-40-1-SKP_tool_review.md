Yes — **if a front axle seal goes bad**, especially on a **BMW E90** (whether it's RWD or xDrive/AWD), **it can absolutely spray differential or transmission fluid** all over the lower part of the car.

Here’s how it happens and what to expect:

---

### 🛠️ What Are Front Axle Seals?
The front axle (also known as the **CV shaft** or **drive shaft**) passes through seals at the transmission or front differential to keep **gear oil or ATF** inside.

- On **RWD E90**: The seals are in the **manual/automatic transmission output** area
- On **xDrive (AWD)**: They are in the **front differential housing** (attached to oil pan area)

---

### 🔧 If They Go Bad:
- **They leak** — small leaks may drip slowly at first
- **Moderate to severe leaks can spray**:
  - Onto the **oil pan**
  - **Belly pan**
  - **Control arms**
  - Even **exhaust pipes** (burning oil smell)

---

### 🚨 Signs Your Axle Seals Are Leaking:
1. **Oily mess** around where the axle enters the trans/diff
2. **Drips** on the garage floor near front tires
3. **Oil smell** from under the hood or firewall area
4. If AWD — **low front diff fluid** can lead to damage or noise

---

### 💥 Will It Spray All Over the Lower End?
- **Yes, especially while driving.** The axle is rotating at thousands of RPM — so if the seal is leaking, it *slings oil outward* like a sprinkler.
- You’ll get **oil coating the subframe, splash shield, engine mount arm, and possibly even the side of the block**.

Here’s what the mess looks like:
- Light film = early failure
- Dripping/slinging = bad seal or dislodged axle

---

### 🧰 What To Do:
- **Inspect both sides** — leaks can be symmetrical
- **Check fluid level** in diff or trans — top off if low
- **Replace the seal(s)** ASAP if you see slinging

It’s a relatively **cheap part**, but labor can be annoying depending on which seal:
- **RWD trans output seal** – ~1 hr per side
- **xDrive front diff seal** – more complex, axle removal required

---

### 💡 Tip:
If the leak is *sudden*, make sure the **axle circlip is fully seated** — a poorly seated CV axle can mimic a failed seal by letting fluid out around it.
