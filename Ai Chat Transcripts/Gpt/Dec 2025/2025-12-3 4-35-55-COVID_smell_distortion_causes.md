## ASSISTANT
What you’re describing is **very common after COVID** and fits with a condition called **parosmia**, a type of smell distortion that can happen when the olfactory system is healing.

Here’s what’s likely going on:

## 🧠 **Why onions smell “wrong” now**
COVID can damage the **olfactory receptor neurons** in the nose. As they regrow, the brain can receive *distorted* or incomplete smell signals. Foods like **onions, garlic, coffee, meat, and chocolate** often become the most distorted because their normal smell is made of many complex molecules.  
When the brain gets scrambled or partial input, it tries to interpret it—often resulting in a smell that is:

- burnt  
- chemical  
- rotten  
- overly sweet  
- like onions or sulfur  

This is why your **onion smell changed**.

## 💦 **Why your sweat smells like onions**
This is another parosmia effect. Your sweat probably *doesn’t actually smell different*, but your **brain is interpreting the odor as “onion-like.”**  
Some people also experience **phantosmia**—smelling something that isn’t there—especially onion, smoke, or chemical smells.

## ✔️ Is this normal?
Yes—post-COVID smell distortion is extremely common. Studies show:

- Up to **40–50%** of people with COVID-related smell loss develop parosmia as they recover.
- It can last **months to years**, but many people improve with time.

## 🧪 What actually causes it (in simple terms)
- COVID injures olfactory neurons.
- They regrow, but their wiring can be “misconnected” at first.
- The brain receives mismatched signals → smell distortion.

It’s similar to hearing static when a radio is partly tuned.

## 🩺 When to get checked
See an ENT (ear, nose, throat doctor) if you have:

- symptoms getting *worse* after a year  
- constant phantom smells (burning, smoke)  
- sinus issues, blockage, or infections  
- severe impact on eating or weight loss  

## 👍 Things that may help
- **Smell training** (clinically recommended): sniffing 4 scents twice daily  
- **Good hydration + nasal hygiene** (saline rinses)  
- **Avoid trigger foods temporarily**  
- Some ENTs may suggest omega‑3s or topical steroids, but this requires medical guidance
