---

### 🔧 **What Is It?**
The charcoal canister is part of the **EVAP (Evaporative Emissions Control)** system. It captures fuel vapors from the gas tank and stores them in charcoal. Later, these vapors are purged and burned in the engine.

On many E36 models (especially U.S.-spec cars), the canister is located in the trunk, under the spare tire well or near it.

---

### ⚠️ **What Happens If You Remove It?**

#### 1. **Evaporative Emissions Codes or CEL (Check Engine Light)**
- On **OBD2** E36s (1996+), removing the canister **without proper tuning or sensor trickery** will likely trigger a fault code and CEL for the EVAP system.
- Common codes: **P0440**, **P0441**, etc.

#### 2. **Fuel Vapor Smell**
- Without a proper vapor management system, **raw fuel vapors** can escape from the tank, especially on hot days.
- This can make the cabin or trunk smell like gas.

#### 3. **Legal/Inspection Issues**
- In areas with emissions testing (especially California or states using **CARB** guidelines), this is **illegal** and may cause your car to fail inspection.

#### 4. **Minimal Performance Gain**
- There is **no performance benefit** to removing the charcoal canister. It doesn’t restrict power, air flow, or weight in any meaningful way.

#### 5. **Potential Vacuum Leak or Driveability Issues**
- If not properly blocked off or routed, removing it can cause **vacuum leaks**, poor idle, or **fuel tank pressure issues**.

---

### ✅ **If You Still Want to Remove It:**
Here’s what you should do to avoid issues:
- **Cap or vent the tank properly** using a small vent filter or routed line.
- **Use a tune** to disable the EVAP codes on OBD2 cars.
- On **OBD1 cars (1992–1995 in the U.S.)**, you can get away with removing it more easily, but still risk vapor smell and legality issues.

---

### 🔚 Conclusion:
Removing the charcoal canister is **not recommended unless you’re doing a track-only build** or live in a place with no emissions enforcement. Even then, do it **properly** to avoid drivability issues and fuel vapor hazards.

Yes — **if your E36 already smells like gas**, especially during **cold starts**, that can point to **a problem with the EVAP system**, and **the charcoal canister could be a prime suspect**.

---

### 🔎 Here's what's likely going on:

1. **Charcoal canister saturated or leaking**
   - If the canister is **full of fuel**, cracked, or its **vent lines are brittle or disconnected**, vapors will escape — particularly noticeable in **cold weather** when the purge system hasn't started working yet.

2. **Purge valve not functioning**
   - The **purge valve** (often near the intake manifold) is supposed to open after the engine warms up. If it's stuck open or closed, vapors can build up or leak.

3. **Leaky fuel lines or injector seals**
   - Especially on older E36s, **rubber fuel lines** under the hood or in the trunk can **crack** and let out vapor — even if the canister is fine.

4. **Fuel tank pressure issues**
   - A **bad gas cap** or **vent valve** can cause pressure imbalances, which might vent raw fuel vapors out through the filler neck or canister lines.

---

### 🥶 Why does it smell more in cold weather?

- In cold temps, the **engine takes longer to enter closed loop**, so the **EVAP purge system is delayed**.
- If vapors are building up and the purge valve hasn’t opened yet, they have nowhere to go — so you **smell it** until the engine warms and starts pulling them into the intake.
- Also, cold plastic and rubber components are more likely to **shrink or leak slightly**.

---

- Open the trunk and remove the spare tire — inspect the **charcoal canister and lines**:
  - Are any lines cracked or disconnected?
  - Can you smell gas directly from the canister?
- Check the **rubber fuel lines** near the fuel rail in the engine bay and in the trunk under the cover.
- Replace the **gas cap** if it’s old or not sealing well.
