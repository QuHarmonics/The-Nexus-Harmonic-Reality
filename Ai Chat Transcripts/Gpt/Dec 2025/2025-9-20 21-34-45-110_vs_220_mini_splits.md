Great question. “110 vs 220” (really 120 V vs 240 V in the U.S.) on mini-splits boils down to a few practical differences:

**1) Capacity & model choices**
- **120 V units** are usually the smaller sizes (typically 6k–12k BTU, sometimes 15k). Mostly single-zone.
- **240 V units** cover **everything** (small to large, multi-zone), and include more “cold-climate / hyper-heat” models.

**2) Current draw (amps) & wire run**
- Power is volts × amps. For the **same wattage**, **240 V draws ~½ the current** of 120 V.  
  (e.g., 1,500 W ≈ 12.5 A at 120 V vs ~6.25 A at 240 V.)
- Lower current means **less voltage drop** on longer wire runs and often **smaller wire gauge**.

**3) Breaker & panel realities**
- **120 V** units often land on a **15–20 A single-pole** breaker.
- **240 V** units use a **two-pole** breaker, commonly **15–30 A** depending on size.
- If your panel is tight on space or you don’t have an easy path for a new 240 V circuit, a 120 V small unit can be simpler—**but both** typically require a **dedicated circuit** and an **outdoor disconnect**.

**4) Start-up & reliability**
- Modern mini-splits are **inverter/soft-start**, so “hard starting” isn’t a big worry at either voltage.  
- That said, **240 V handles long runs and saggy household voltage better**, which can help stability.

**5) Efficiency & operating cost**
- **Energy cost is about the same** for the same SEER/SEER2 and load. Voltage doesn’t change kWh; **model efficiency does**.
- At small sizes, 120 V vs 240 V efficiency is usually similar. At larger sizes and in cold-climate lines, **240 V has more high-efficiency options**.

**6) Cold-climate performance (Detroit winters)**
- If you want real **heating** performance (low-ambient, “hyper-heat” down to sub-zero °F), you’ll find **far more choices at 240 V**.  
- Many 120 V units de-rate faster as temps drop and are best thought of as cooling-first or mild-heat.
