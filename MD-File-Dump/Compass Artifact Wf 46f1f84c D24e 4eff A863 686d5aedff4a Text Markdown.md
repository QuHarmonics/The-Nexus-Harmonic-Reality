# Spectral universality across substrates: a unified computational geometry

**The same eigenvalue statistics govern systems from Riemann zeta zeros to protein vibrations to turbulent fluids — and this cross-domain universality carries direct implications for the Clay Millennium Problems.** The Montgomery-Odlyzko law, the Bohigas-Giannoni-Schmit conjecture, and the Kolmogorov energy spectrum all instantiate a single mathematical phenomenon: deterministic systems with sufficient complexity produce spectral signatures indistinguishable from random matrix ensembles. This report compiles the precise formulas, empirical evidence, computational methods, and recent (2020–2026) breakthroughs needed to construct a unified paper connecting substrate-independent geometric computation to spectral universality, with implications for Riemann Hypothesis, Navier-Stokes regularity, and Yang-Mills mass gap.

## 1. GUE pair correlation and the Montgomery-Odlyzko law

The foundational formula connecting number theory to random matrix theory is Montgomery's pair correlation conjecture (1973). Assuming the Riemann Hypothesis, the normalized pair correlation function of zeta zeros is:

$$R_2(u) = 1 - \left(\frac{\sin \pi u}{\pi u}\right)^2$$

where $u$ is the normalized spacing between zeros $\frac{1}{2} + i\gamma$, $\frac{1}{2} + i\gamma'$, rescaled by the mean spacing $2\pi/\log T$. Freeman Dyson immediately recognized this as the GUE two-point correlation — the sine-kernel determinantal formula. Montgomery proved the result for the Fourier transform $F(\alpha)$ in the range $|\alpha| < 1$ and conjectured $F(\alpha) = 1$ for $|\alpha| \geq 1$.

**Exact GUE statistics needed for comparison.** The Wigner surmise for GUE nearest-neighbor spacing is $p_{\text{GUE}}(s) = \frac{32}{\pi^2}s^2 \exp(-\frac{4}{\pi}s^2)$, accurate to ~2–5% against the exact Gaudin-Mehta distribution expressed via Painlevé V transcendents. The spectral form factor exhibits a characteristic linear ramp: $K_{\text{GUE}}(\tau) = \tau$ for $\tau \leq 1$, plateauing at $K = 1$ for $\tau \geq 1$. The number variance grows logarithmically: $\Sigma^2(L) = \frac{2}{\pi^2}[\ln(2\pi L) + \gamma + 1 - \frac{\pi^2}{8}] + O(L^{-1})$, where $\gamma \approx 0.5772$ is the Euler-Mascheroni constant. This logarithmic growth — versus linear for Poisson — is the hallmark of **spectral rigidity** with Dyson index $\beta = 2$.

**Odlyzko's numerical verification** computed billions of consecutive zeros at heights up to $10^{23}$, finding stunning agreement with GUE predictions for pair correlation, nearest-neighbor spacing, number variance, and spectral rigidity. Gourdon extended computations to $2 \times 10^9$ zeros near the $10^{24}$-th zero. Forrester and Odlyzko (2015) characterized the $O(1/N^2)$ finite-size corrections using Painlevé transcendents, achieving even closer agreement.

**Recent breakthroughs (2020–2026)** include Goldston, Schettler, Suriajaya, and Turnage-Butterbaugh (2025) showing that the pair correlation conjecture — without assuming RH — implies asymptotically 100% of zeros are both simple and on the critical line. Jerby (2025, arXiv:2511.18275) claims a proof of the pair correlation conjecture for Hardy's Z-function using Dyson Brownian motion with $\beta = 2$. The Katz-Sarnak framework extends universality to all automorphic L-functions, with the symmetry type (Unitary, Symplectic, Orthogonal) determined by the arithmetic of each family. Griffin, Ono, Rolen, and Zagier (2019, PNAS) rigorously showed Jensen polynomials for the zeta function satisfy GUE predictions after renormalization. Quantum simulation experiments have detected Riemann zeros using trapped ions (Okada et al., 2021, npj Quantum Information) and 5-qubit spin systems (Wei et al., 2025).

## 2. Spectral universality spans quantum and classical boundaries

The Bohigas-Giannoni-Schmit conjecture (1984) asserts that quantum systems whose classical limit is chaotic exhibit GOE/GUE eigenvalue statistics, depending on time-reversal symmetry. This conjecture remains unproven but is supported by the Sieber-Richter periodic orbit mechanism (2001) and the Müller-Heusler-Braun-Haake-Altland reconstruction (2004–2009) of the full spectral form factor from correlated orbit pairs — reproducing all terms in the $\tau$-expansion of $K(\tau)$. The complementary Berry-Tabor conjecture holds that integrable systems produce Poisson statistics; Marklof and Welsh (January 2025) extended rigorous proofs to 3-dimensional tori.

**Cross-domain universality is the empirical centerpiece.** GUE/GOE statistics have been confirmed in: nuclear resonances (Wigner's original motivation), quantum dots, microwave billiard experiments, the QCD Dirac operator (Shuryak and Verbaarschot, 1993), complex networks (Bandyopadhyay and Jalan, 2007 — scale-free, small-world, and random graphs all show GOE nearest-neighbor spacing), financial correlation matrices (Laloux et al., 2000 — bulk eigenvalues follow Marchenko-Pastur with outlier signal modes), protein-protein interaction networks across six species (Jalan, 2015), gene co-expression networks with a sharp GOE→Poisson transition at meaningful correlation thresholds (Luo et al., 2007), and — remarkably — bird nest spacings in ecological systems (Akemann et al., fitting $\beta$-ensembles with $\beta > 0$). Bus arrival spacings in Cuernavaca, Mexico showed GUE-like level repulsion.

**Rigorous universality theorems** from the Erdős-Schlein-Yau program establish bulk universality for Wigner matrices via Dyson Brownian motion local relaxation flow, proving eigenvalue rigidity: bulk eigenvalues lie within $O(N^{-1})$ of their classical locations. Tao and Vu's Four Moment Theorem and Lindeberg replacement strategy independently proved the Wigner-Dyson-Mehta conjecture — for all Wigner matrices with finite moments, $k$-point correlation functions converge to the Dyson sine-kernel determinantal form. Extensions include non-Hermitian universality (Cipolloni, Erdős, Schröder), two new non-Hermitian classes (Hamazaki et al., 2020), and $\beta$-ensembles for continuous Dyson index (Dumitriu and Edelman, 2002).

The physical mechanism behind universality is **level repulsion**: the joint eigenvalue distribution $P(\lambda_1, \ldots, \lambda_N) \propto \prod_{i<j} |\lambda_i - \lambda_j|^\beta \cdot e^{-\beta \sum \lambda_i^2/4}$ forces the Vandermonde factor to suppress degeneracies. Dyson's Coulomb gas analogy maps eigenvalues to charged particles with logarithmic repulsion at inverse temperature $\beta$.

## 3. Protein eigenvalue spectra sit at a critical point

Elastic Network Models (ENMs) provide the primary spectral framework for proteins. The Gaussian Network Model constructs an $N \times N$ Kirchhoff matrix $\Gamma$ from $C_\alpha$ contacts within cutoff $r_c \approx 7$ Å; the Anisotropic Network Model builds a $3N \times 3N$ Hessian $H$. B-factors follow $B_i = \frac{8\pi^2 k_B T}{\gamma}[\Gamma^{-1}]_{ii}$, achieving correlations $\sim$0.55–0.60 with experimental data.

**Tang and Kaneko (2020, Phys. Rev. Research)** demonstrated across **9,166 proteins** that vibration spectra follow a power-law distribution $\lambda_1 \sim N^{-\zeta}$ with $\zeta \approx 1$, intermediate between ordered lattices ($\zeta \approx 0.73$) and ideal polymers ($\zeta \approx 1.67$). This power-law signature indicates **criticality** — proteins occupy a dynamical regime balanced between robustness and plasticity. Correlation lengths extend across entire proteins regardless of size. The spectral dimension $d_s \approx 4/3$ (confirmed by NMR relaxation) yields a vibrational density of states $\sigma(\omega) \propto \omega^{d_s - 1}$, dramatically enhanced at low frequencies relative to crystalline models.

**Random matrix theory meets protein dynamics.** Potestio et al. (Phys. Rev. Lett., 2009) applied RMT to amino acid displacement covariance matrices and found bulk universality in local spacing statistics, well-described by a **Brody distribution with $\beta \approx 0.8$** — intermediate between Poisson ($\beta = 0$) and Wigner-Dyson ($\beta = 1$). Eigenvalues exceeding the Marchenko-Pastur upper edge $\lambda_+ = \sigma^2(1 + \sqrt{N/M})^2$ separate genuine collective motions from noise, providing a rigorous signal-noise criterion superior to variance thresholds. Power spectral density of atomic variables varies as **$1/f^\alpha$** over wide frequency ranges (Ozkan and Bhatt, 2013, PLOS Comp. Biol.), with phase clustering identifying collectively fluctuating residues.

Persistent spectral theory (Wei group, 2022–2025) goes beyond standard persistent homology by extracting non-harmonic Laplacian spectra. The TopLapNet framework successfully predicted SARS-CoV-2 variants BA.4/BA.5 before WHO announcement. ProFlex (2025, Nature Communications) applies NMA to hundreds of thousands of AlphaFold structures at scale, defining a flexibility alphabet.

## 4. Cryptographic spectral properties and the random oracle boundary

SHA-256 spectral analysis reveals quantitative departures from ideal randomness. Vaughn and Borowczak (2024) measured that the **SHA-256 compression function does not strictly satisfy the Strict Avalanche Criterion** — SAC mean reaches ~50% by round 23 of 64 but confidence intervals never contain exactly 0.5. The function exhibits the avalanche effect but technically lacks completeness. The CHOOSE sub-function, carry operation, and $\Sigma_1$ contribute most to diffusion; removing the message scheduler has minimal impact.

**The spectral framework for block cipher analysis** was transformed by Beyne's work (ASIACRYPT 2018, Journal of Cryptology 2020): block cipher invariants are **eigenvectors of correlation matrices**, with eigenvalues that are roots of unity for permutations. This unifies linear, differential, integral, and zero-correlation cryptanalysis through a single spectral lens. For the AES S-box: nonlinearity is **112** (maximum for balanced 8-bit Boolean functions is 116), differential uniformity is 4, and Walsh spectrum maximum determines linear approximation probability. The MDS MixColumns matrix achieves optimal branch number **5**, guaranteeing minimum active S-boxes per round.

**Direct RMT applications to cryptography remain nascent but theoretically motivated.** For an ideal random permutation, the correlation matrix should resemble a random orthogonal matrix, so eigenvalue spacings of a "good" cipher should follow GOE/GUE statistics. Deviations from universal spacing statistics could indicate exploitable structure — this remains an open research direction. SHA-256 passes all 15 NIST SP 800-22 tests, including the DFT/spectral test, though the test suite itself has been criticized as obsolete (IACR ePrint 2022/169). Neural network attacks show no meaningful advantage beyond ~4 rounds of SHA-256; best public cryptanalysis breaks 52/64 rounds (preimage) and 46/64 rounds (collision).

## 5. Persistent homology captures multiscale topological evolution

Persistent homology extracts topological features (connected components $\beta_0$, loops $\beta_1$, cavities $\beta_2$) across filtration scales. The bottleneck stability theorem guarantees $W_\infty(\text{Dgm}(f), \text{Dgm}(g)) \leq \|f - g\|_\infty$, making TDA robust to noise. CROCKER plots — Contour Realization Of Computed k-dimensional hole Evolution (Topaz, Ziegelmeier, Halverson, 2015) — display $\beta_k(t, \varepsilon)$ as 2D contour plots over time $t$ and scale $\varepsilon$ simultaneously, achieving **93.1% classification accuracy** on swarming agent models where classical order parameters achieve only 46.7%.

**For dynamical systems**, Takens embedding converts time series to point clouds, where periodic signals produce loops (prominent $H_1$), quasiperiodic signals produce tori ($H_2$), and chaotic signals produce diffuse clouds. The **persistence exponent** (Krishnamoorthy and Thompson, January 2026) is a provably stable chaos measure running in $O(N^2 \log N)$, experimentally more stable than the Lyapunov exponent. CROCKER stacks (Adams et al., FODS 2022) add $\alpha$-smoothing for noise filtering with proven continuity properties.

**Molecular TDA applications** include protein folding analysis via topological feature vectors (Ichinomiya et al., 2020 — identifying native, misfolded, transition, and unfolded states), drug design via TopNetTree (Wei group), and analysis of all **214 million AlphaFold structures** using persistent homology (Nature Communications, 2025). Vectorization methods — persistence images (Adams et al., JMLR 2017), persistence landscapes (Bubenik, 2015), PersLay neural network layers (Carrière et al., AISTATS 2020) — enable integration with machine learning. Key software: Ripser (~40× faster than alternatives), GUDHI, Giotto-TDA, Dionysus 2.

## 6. Fisher-Rao geometry unifies thermodynamics and computation

The Fisher information matrix $g_{ij}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}[\partial_i \log p \cdot \partial_j \log p]$ is, by **Čencov's theorem, the unique Riemannian metric invariant under sufficient statistics**. The Gaussian parameter space $(\mu, \sigma)$ under Fisher-Rao is isometric to the Poincaré upper half-plane with constant negative curvature $R = -1$.

**The thermodynamic connection is exact.** Ito (2018, Phys. Rev. Lett.) proved that **entropy production rate equals half the temporal Fisher information**: $\dot{\sigma} = \frac{1}{2}F(t)$ for Fokker-Planck dynamics. Ito and Dechant (2020, Phys. Rev. X) established speed limits: $|\frac{d\langle O\rangle}{dt}| \leq \sqrt{\text{Var}(O)} \cdot \sqrt{F(t)}$. The Salamon-Berry thermodynamic length $L = \int \sqrt{\dot{\theta}^T g \dot{\theta}} \, dt$ satisfies a minimum dissipation bound $\langle W_{\text{diss}}\rangle \geq L^2/(2\tau)$, achieved by constant-speed geodesics on the Fisher-Rao manifold. Sivak and Crooks (2012) derived the friction tensor linking Fisher information to relaxation times, showing optimal protocols are geodesics. The connection to Wasserstein distance was established by Nakazato and Ito (2021): entropy production is bounded by Wasserstein action, unified by the HWI inequality $H \leq W_2\sqrt{I} - \frac{\kappa}{2}W_2^2$.

**For computation**, the natural gradient $\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta G^{-1}\nabla L$ (Amari, 1998) provides parameterization-invariant optimization. K-FAC approximates the Fisher matrix via Kronecker products for efficient $O(n^2)$ inversion. Score matching (Hyvärinen, 2005) estimates $\nabla \log p$ without normalization, underpinning diffusion generative models. Zhang (2025) proved the cosine schedule for diffusion models is uniquely optimal in Fisher-Rao metric. Practical Fisher estimation from data uses empirical score functions, neural estimators (FINE, 2023), or Stein's identity, with bias corrections critical for finite samples (Kanitscheider et al., 2015). Quantum Fisher information extends to metrology, with the quantum Cramér-Rao bound $\text{Var}(\hat{\theta}) \geq 1/(N \cdot F_Q)$ governing Heisenberg-limited sensing.

## 7. Circuit topology decomposes folded chains into S, P, X motifs

Mashaghi, van Wijk, and Tans (Structure, 2014) introduced circuit topology by classifying all pairwise contact arrangements on a linear chain into three types: **Series (S)** — disjoint intervals, string notation AABB; **Parallel (P)** — nested intervals, ABBA; **Cross (X)** — interleaved intervals, ABAB. This exhausts all topological possibilities for binary contacts and produces an $n \times n$ SPX matrix fingerprinting any fold.

**Key properties**: P is transitive but S and X are not. Cross contacts correspond to RNA pseudoknots. The genus of a fold diagram relates to the count and arrangement of X contacts — folds with only S and P are planar. Flapan, Mashaghi, and Wong (2023, Scientific Reports) proved via a tile model that only knots $3_1, 4_1, 5_1, 5_2, 6_1, 6_2, 6_3, 7_7, 8_{12}$ and connected sums are obtainable — **including all known protein knots**. Polynomial invariants (Mashaghi and van der Veen, 2021) and Jones polynomial calculations via braid-theoretic translation (Berx and Mashaghi, 2023, Soft Matter) provide computable invariants distinguishing circuit topologies.

The framework has been applied to **the entire human proteome** via ProteinCT software (GitHub: circuittopology/circuit_topology), operating on conventional hardware in hours. SPX fractions predict folding kinetics (Scalvini et al., 2021 — number of circuits correlates with folding rate), mechanical unfolding pathways (Nikoofard and Mashaghi, 2018), and missense mutation pathogenicity (Woodard et al., 2022). Extensions beyond biology include chromatin topology from Hi-C data (Scalvini et al., 2022), polymer entanglement, and — crucially — **semantic topology for text analysis** (ACL 2025), demonstrating the framework applies to logical chains, not just physical polymers. Mashaghi and Ramezanpour (2015) explicitly note applicability to "economics, computer science, social science."

## 8. Navier-Stokes Turing completeness and the regularity impasse

**Cardona, Miranda, Peralta-Salas, and Presas (PNAS 2021)** proved that stationary solutions to the 3D incompressible Euler equations on a Riemannian $S^3$ can simulate any Turing machine — making it undecidable whether a trajectory from an explicit point reaches an explicit open set. The proof uses Beltrami fields (eigenvectors of curl), the Etnyre-Ghrist correspondence with Reeb flows on contact manifolds, and an h-principle for Reeb embeddings. In **July 2025**, Enciso, Peñafiel-Tomás, and Peralta-Salas extended this to **stationary Navier-Stokes** on manifolds with nonvanishing harmonic 1-forms, showing viscosity does not obstruct computational universality given appropriate geometry.

**The regularity landscape has shifted dramatically.** Chen and Hou (2022, published PNAS 2025) rigorously proved finite-time singularity formation for 3D axisymmetric Euler equations with smooth data and cylindrical boundary, using computer-assisted stable self-similar blowup analysis. Elgindi (Ann. Math. 2021) showed finite-time singularity for $C^{1,\alpha}$ Euler solutions on $\mathbb{R}^3$. On the flexibility side, Isett (2018) resolved the Onsager conjecture: weak solutions in $C^{0,\alpha}$ with $\alpha < 1/3$ can dissipate energy (connecting to Kolmogorov's $k^{-5/3}$ spectrum where velocity increments scale as $\ell^{1/3}$). Buckmaster and Vicol (2019) proved non-uniqueness of finite-energy weak Navier-Stokes solutions via convex integration with intermittency. Non-uniqueness of smooth NS solutions from BMO$^{-1}$ critical data appeared in Inventiones Math. (2025).

**The computation-regularity tension is profound.** Tao's program (2014–present) proposed constructing blow-up by building a "fluid computer" — self-replicating logic gates concentrating energy to singularity. As Kalai observed, if NS equations cannot support fault-tolerant computation, this limitation might itself constitute a proof of regularity. The Kolmogorov energy spectrum $E(k) = C_K \varepsilon^{2/3} k^{-5/3}$ (with universal constant $C_K \approx 1.5$) represents spectral universality in turbulence, independent of forcing details — paralleling RMT universality.

## 9. Yang-Mills mass gap through confinement and spectral geometry

The Clay problem requires constructing quantum Yang-Mills on $\mathbb{R}^4$ for compact simple gauge group $G$ satisfying Wightman axioms, with mass gap $\Delta > 0$ — the lightest particle has strictly positive mass. The problem remains **open as of February 2026** (Douglas, Nature Reviews Physics 8, 2026).

**Lattice QCD provides the strongest numerical evidence.** Athenodorou and Teper (2020–2021) computed the most comprehensive glueball spectrum: scalar $0^{++}$ at **1610 ± 150 MeV** (~3.5$\sqrt{\sigma}$ where $\sqrt{\sigma} \approx 420$ MeV), tensor $2^{++}$ at **2260 ± 220 MeV**, pseudoscalar $0^{-+}$ at **2560 ± 130 MeV**. The ratio $m(2^{++})/m(0^{++}) \approx 1.4$–$1.5$ is stable across calculations and gauge groups SU(2) through SU(12). The gluon propagator saturates in the infrared at $\Delta^{-1}(0) \approx (500 \text{ MeV})^{-2}$, violating positivity (no Källén-Lehmann representation) — confirming gluon confinement.

**The Schwinger mechanism for dynamical gluon mass** is the most actively studied framework (Aguilar, Binosi, Papavassiliou, 2020–2025). Nonperturbative massless composite poles in the three-gluon vertex trigger mass generation while preserving gauge invariance. Ferreira and Papavassiliou (2024) showed the Fredholm alternative theorem enforces masslessness in linear approximation, but nonlinearity evades this, yielding effective gluon mass $m_g \approx 350$–$500$ MeV. Lattice data confirmed "with extremely high confidence" the presence of dynamical massless bound-state excitations in the dressed three-gluon vertex (Aguilar et al., 2023).

**Spectral connections through chiral RMT.** The Banks-Casher relation $\langle\bar{q}q\rangle = -\pi\rho(0)/V$ connects chiral symmetry breaking to the Dirac spectral density at zero. Verbaarschot (1993–1994) showed Dirac eigenvalues near zero follow chiral random matrix theory with three universality classes (chiral orthogonal, unitary, symplectic), matching Dyson's classification. The microscopic spectral density $\rho_s(z) = \frac{z}{2}[J_\nu^2(z) - J_{\nu+1}(z)J_{\nu-1}(z)]$ is confirmed extensively on the lattice. Pandey (2023, JHEP) proposed a geometric approach using Bakry-Émery Ricci curvature on the orbit space $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{G}$: positive curvature produces a spectral gap for the Hamiltonian, with dimensional transmutation in 3+1D.

## 10. Constraint satisfaction thresholds exhibit universal spectral phase transitions

The **Ding-Sly-Sun theorem** (Annals of Mathematics, 2022) proved that for large $k$, random $k$-SAT has a sharp satisfiability threshold given by the 1-RSB prediction: $\alpha_{\text{SAT}}(k) = 2^k \ln 2 - \frac{1}{2}(1 + \ln 2) + o_k(1)$. For 3-SAT, $\alpha_c \approx 4.267$. The solution space undergoes a cascade: replica symmetric → clustering ($\alpha_d \approx 3.86$ for 3-SAT) → condensation → rigidity/freezing ($\alpha_f \approx 4.254$) → unsatisfiable. Bresler and Huang (2021) proved low-degree polynomial hardness within a constant factor of the best algorithmic threshold via the multi-overlap gap property.

**The BBP phase transition is the universal spectral signature of computational thresholds.** For spiked random matrices $Y = \lambda xx^T/\sqrt{N} + W$, the Baik-Ben Arous-Péché transition at $\lambda = 1$ determines whether the top eigenvalue separates from the semicircle bulk. Below threshold, fluctuations follow the **Tracy-Widom distribution** (spectral methods fail); above, Gaussian fluctuations emerge and detection succeeds. This same transition governs: community detection in stochastic block models at the Kesten-Stigum bound $d\lambda^2 = 1$ (Massoulié, 2014; Mossel-Neeman-Sly, 2013), tensor PCA, planted clique at $k \approx \sqrt{n}$, and sparse PCA.

**Information-computation gaps are universal.** The gap between information-theoretic and computational thresholds appears across planted clique (IT: $k \approx 2\log_2 n$, computational: $k \approx \sqrt{n}$), sparse PCA (IT: SNR $\sim 1/\sqrt{p}$, computational: SNR $\sim 1$), and random $k$-SAT (IT: $\alpha \sim 2^k \ln 2$, computational: $\alpha \sim 2^k \ln k / k$). The **low-degree polynomial framework** (Schramm and Wein, Ann. Stat. 2022) provides a unified prediction: for $D \approx \log n$, the low-degree likelihood ratio diverges in the "easy" regime and remains $O(1)$ in the "hard" regime, matching all known thresholds. The overlap gap property (Gamarnik, PNAS 2021) provides topological barriers to stable algorithms.

The Parisi formula for the SK model (proved by Talagrand, 2006; extended to ultrametricity by Panchenko, 2013) connects the free energy landscape to RSB structure. Backtracking Survey Propagation (Parisi et al., Nature Communications 2016) finds solutions up to $\alpha \approx 4.262$ for 3-SAT in near-linear time — all found solutions have no frozen variables.

## The cross-domain universality architecture

The mathematical structures connecting all ten domains form a coherent framework:

**Spectral universality as the common thread.** GUE statistics appear in Riemann zeros ($R_2(u) = 1 - \text{sinc}^2(\pi u)$), nuclear resonances, quantum chaotic systems, complex networks, biological networks (GOE for protein-protein interactions), and QCD Dirac eigenvalues — all governed by Dyson's $\beta$ parameter and the Vandermonde level repulsion mechanism. Protein dynamics sit at criticality with Brody parameter $\beta \approx 0.8$, intermediate between Poisson and Wigner-Dyson. The Kolmogorov $k^{-5/3}$ spectrum provides spectral universality in turbulence.

**Information geometry as the unifying metric.** Fisher-Rao geometry connects thermodynamic dissipation ($\dot{\sigma} = \frac{1}{2}F(t)$) to computational optimization (natural gradient descent) to molecular dynamics (optimal protocols as geodesics). Čencov's uniqueness theorem ensures the Fisher metric appears in any information-theoretic setting with sufficient-statistic invariance. The HWI inequality universally connects entropy, transport, and Fisher information.

**Topological invariants bridge scales.** Circuit topology's SPX decomposition classifies fold structures from proteins to text to computational graphs. Persistent homology tracks topological evolution across filtration scales, with CROCKER plots providing simultaneous time-scale analysis. The persistence exponent (2026) offers a stable chaos measure.

**Computational universality meets spectral gaps.** Euler flows are Turing complete (Cardona et al., 2021); stationary Navier-Stokes achieves Turing completeness on appropriate manifolds (Enciso et al., July 2025). The Yang-Mills mass gap is a spectral gap. The BBP transition in random matrices determines computational thresholds for constraint satisfaction. Phase transitions in $k$-SAT mirror thermodynamic transitions in spin glasses, with RSB structure determining algorithmic tractability.

## Conclusion: the research frontier

Three convergences define the path forward. First, the GUE universality class appears to be the spectral signature of "sufficient computational complexity" — whether in number-theoretic sequences, chaotic dynamical systems, or biological networks. Extracting eigenvalue spectra from SHA-256 round-function correlation matrices and comparing spacing statistics against GUE predictions represents an unexplored empirical test. Second, Fisher-Rao geometry provides a metric framework unifying thermodynamic cost, computational efficiency, and dynamical optimization, with entropy production as the bridge quantity. Third, the Turing completeness results for fluid equations create a direct mathematical channel between computational universality and the Clay regularity problem: proving NS regularity requires demonstrating structural limitations on the equations' computational capacity, while spectral gap results (Yang-Mills mass gap, BBP transition) provide the mathematical vocabulary for such limitations. The overlap gap property, proven as a topological barrier to algorithms in constraint satisfaction, may provide templates for understanding spectral barriers in gauge theory and fluid dynamics.

The reproducible computational pipeline requires: Ripser/GUDHI for persistent homology, ProDy/Bio3D for protein normal modes, NIST SP 800-22 plus Walsh-Hadamard analysis for cryptographic spectra, Geomstats for Fisher-Rao geodesics, and ProteinCT for circuit topology — all open-source and available for cross-domain spectral universality testing against the GUE prediction curves compiled here.
