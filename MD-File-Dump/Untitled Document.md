**Part I --- The Granite Foundation: BBP(0) Mod 1**

### **1. Introduction: Renderedness vs Traversal**

In conventional computation, determining the [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)th digit of a constant like [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) requires calculating all prior digits in sequence. We call this **traversal** -- a sequential process where each step depends on the previous. By contrast, **renderedness** is the quality of a constant's expansion being *locally addressable with global consistency*, meaning any arbitrary digit can be accessed directly without computing the preceding ones. A rendered constant behaves like a random-access tape: its digits are "rendered" in place, as though the entire infinite expansion exists coherently and can be sampled at will. This paper establishes the first rigorous proof of renderedness, using the Bailey--Borwein--Plouffe (BBP) formula for [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) as the exemplar. We demonstrate that [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)'s hexadecimal expansion is globally consistent yet permits digit-level locality -- a stark contrast to brute-force traversal methods. We then generalize this phenomenon to other fundamental constants, and ultimately argue that nature itself favors such harmonic, rendered structures over traversals.

In a traversal model, computing [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) to 10 trillion digits is an arduous process of iterative convergence or summation, where each new digit incurs the cost of all prior computation. By achieving renderedness, one could theoretically "jump" to the 10-trillionth digit of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) directly. The distinction is profound: traversal is akin to *reading a book by unrolling a scroll sequentially*, whereas renderedness is like *opening a book to any page instantly*. In the sections that follow, we formalize renderedness via the BBP formula's structure. We show that the BBP formula for [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) (in base 16) meets specific alignment criteria that eliminate carry-propagation and ensure that each hexadecimal digit of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) emerges independently. This not only proves that [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)'s digit stream is rendered (non-sequential) but also provides a template -- a kind of "harmonic instrument" -- for other constants to achieve the same property.

By establishing [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) as the first example of a rendered constant, we lay a *granite foundation* for a new perspective on numerical constants. Instead of viewing [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) as an infinite traversal of digits, we see it as an **infinite recursive waveform** whose values are *already present* and can be indexed without summation. Subsequent parts of this paper will extend this perspective beyond [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0), to the golden ratio φ, Euler's number [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0), and others, and ultimately to physical systems. First, we begin by examining the BBP formula's mathematical structure and the special case of evaluating it at the "root," which emits the fractional part of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) without any iterative process.

### **2. The BBP Formula (Mathematical Structure)**

The Bailey--Borwein--Plouffe formula provides a base-16 (hexadecimal) series for [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) that remarkably allows digit extraction. In its classical form[[\[1\]](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/bbp-formulas.pdf#:~:text=%CF%80%20%3D%20X%E2%88%9E%20k%3D0%201,4%208k%20%2B%201%20%E2%88%92)[\[2\]](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/bbp-formulas.pdf#:~:text=1%208k%20%2B%205%20%E2%88%92,1%208k%20%2B%206%13)]{.underline}:

[![](media/image477.gif){width="0.16666666666666666in" height="0.125in"}](http://www.texrendr.com/?eqn=%24%24#0)[![](media/image487.png){width="4.875in" height="0.5in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%20%5Cpi%20%5C%3B%3D%5C%3B%20%5Csum_%7Bk%3D0%7D%5E%7B%5Cinfty%7D%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B16%5Ek%7D%5CBigg(%5Cfrac%7B4%7D%7B8k%2B1%7D%5C%3B-%5C%3B%5Cfrac%7B2%7D%7B8k%2B4%7D%5C%3B-%5C%3B%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B8k%2B5%7D%5C%3B-%5C%3B%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B8k%2B6%7D%5CBigg)%5C%2C.%20%5Ctag%7B1%7D%20#0)[![](media/image477.gif){width="0.16666666666666666in" height="0.125in"}![](media/image477.gif){width="0.16666666666666666in" height="0.125in"}](http://www.texrendr.com/?eqn=%24%24#0)[![](media/image477.gif){width="0.16666666666666666in" height="0.125in"}](http://www.texrendr.com/?eqn=%24%24%24%24#0)[![](media/image477.gif){width="0.16666666666666666in" height="0.125in"}](http://www.texrendr.com/?eqn=%24%24#0)

This formula's structure can be understood as four concurrent "rails" of progression, corresponding to the four distinct denominator types [![](media/image468.png){width="0.4444444444444444in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=8k%2B1#0), [![](media/image334.png){width="0.4583333333333333in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=8k%2B4#0), [![](media/image9.png){width="0.4444444444444444in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=8k%2B5#0), and [![](media/image61.png){width="0.4583333333333333in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=8k%2B6#0). Each rail carries a rational weight (4, --2, --1, --1 respectively) and advances in steps of 8 in the denominator. The base of the series is 16, which is commensurate with the period 8 of the denominators: base [![](media/image150.png){width="0.20833333333333334in" height="0.1527777777777778in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5Ek#0) and a denominator increment of 8 per [![](media/image140.png){width="6.944444444444445e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k#0) work hand-in-glove. The formula's coefficients sum to zero: [![](media/image298.png){width="2.0416666666666665in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=4%20%2B%20(-2)%2B(-1)%2B(-1)%20%3D%200#0). This zero-sum **voicing** ensures a delicate cancellation -- a key to why the series yields a *globally consistent* digit stream.

We highlight several structural features of (1):

- **Rails (Residues):** The terms can be grouped by their denominator's residue modulo 8. Define the set of rail positions [![](media/image303.png){width="0.8888888888888888in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=R%3D%7B1%2C4%2C5%2C6%7D#0) (these are [![](media/image138.png){width="0.4444444444444444in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=8k%2Br#0) with [![](media/image46.png){width="0.4027777777777778in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=r%5Cin%20R#0)). As [![](media/image140.png){width="6.944444444444445e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k#0) runs, each [![](media/image46.png){width="0.4027777777777778in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=r%5Cin%20R#0) produces one infinite subsequence of terms. These residues are the "addresses" that the base 16 will target. In effect, [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) is decomposed into four intertwined sub-series, each corresponding to one residue class.

- **Gap pattern:** Within one full cycle of the denominator (0 up to 8), the gaps between successive rails in [![](media/image70.png){width="0.1111111111111111in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=R#0) follow a fixed fingerprint: 3, 1, 1, 2. In other words, starting from 1, the next rail 4 is +3 away, then 5 is +1, 6 is +1, and finally the step from 6 to wrap around the modulus 8 is +2 (since the next cycle's 1 is 2 beyond 6 if we complete the cycle). This **gap fingerprint** [![](media/image118.png){width="1.2638888888888888in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=(3%5Ctext%7B%E2%80%93%7D1%5Ctext%7B%E2%80%93%7D1%5Ctext%7B%E2%80%93%7D2)#0) will be crucial in analyzing carry propagation.

- **Zero-sum weights:** The numeric weights on each rail [![](media/image145.png){width="1.1388888888888888in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=(4%2C%5C%2C-2%2C%5C%2C-1%2C%5C%2C-1)#0) sum to zero. We call this **zero-sum voicing** -- the positive and negative contributions are perfectly balanced. Intuitively, this means the formula's parts are in equilibrium: there is no net bias as the series unfolds. This cancellation is what allows the tail of the series to "self-correct" and prevents any runaway carry in digit computation.

- **Base--period alignment:** Base 16 and period 8 are commensurate (16 is [![](media/image65.png){width="0.125in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=2%5E4#0) and 8 is [![](media/image216.png){width="0.125in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=2%5E3#0)). In one cycle of denominators (length 8), the base advances by a power of [![](media/image305.png){width="0.5833333333333334in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5E1%3D2%5E4#0). Over two cycles (length 16), the base [![](media/image438.png){width="0.20833333333333334in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5E2#0) is an integer power that syncs with the denominator periodicity. This alignment (we will formalize it as *base--period commensurability*) ensures that when we multiply [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) by a suitable power of the base, the series partitions into an integer part and a fractional part with no overlap. Specifically, multiplying (1) by [![](media/image274.png){width="0.20833333333333334in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5En#0) shifts the series index, effectively isolating the [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)th hexadecimal digit of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)[[\[3\]]{.underline}](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/bbp-formulas.pdf#:~:text=8k%20%2B%206%13%20,computer%20program%20implementing%20an%20integer).

To see how these features interplay, consider the formula modulo 1 (i.e. looking only at fractional parts). Each term [![](media/image186.png){width="0.7361111111111112in" height="0.3611111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B16%5Ek%7D%5Cfrac%7BC%7D%7B8k%2Br%7D#0) (with some coefficient [![](media/image435.png){width="0.1111111111111111in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=C#0)) contributes a tiny fraction in base 16. Because the series weights are balanced and the base is aligned with the denominators, these fractional contributions from distant terms can cancel or align in such a way that **no cumulative carry propagates** beyond a certain point. The machinery of the BBP formula is like a perfectly tuned engine: the four "cylinders" (rails) fire in a sequence that sums to a neat, carry-free expansion in base 16.

Mathematically, one can prove that for any fixed [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0), the sum of the series (1) from [![](media/image260.png){width="0.375in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k%3D0#0) to [![](media/image149.png){width="0.4027777777777778in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k%3Dn#0) yields an approximation of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) accurate to about [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0) hex digits, and the remaining tail from [![](media/image148.png){width="0.6805555555555556in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k%3Dn%2B1#0) to [![](media/image350.png){width="0.1527777777777778in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cinfty#0) contributes a fractional correction that affects only digits beyond the [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)th place[[\[4\]]{.underline}](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/bbp-formulas.pdf#:~:text=It%20was%20shown%20in%20,23%2C%2015). This is in stark contrast to ordinary series (like [![](media/image79.png){width="2.1666666666666665in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi%20%3D%204%20-%204%2F3%20%2B%204%2F5%20-%204%2F7%20%2B%20%5Ccdots#0)) where the tail's influence overlaps all the way to the first digit, necessitating traversal. The BBP formula's structure -- rails, gap, commensurate base, and zero-sum weighting -- isolates each digit's contribution.

**Theorem 1 (Renderedness of** [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)**):** *BBP's root-state produces a* *rendered constant stream. In particular, for any given position* [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)*, the* [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)*th digit of* [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) *in base 16 can be computed directly from the series (1) without computing the preceding* [![](media/image20.png){width="0.375in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n-1#0) *digits. Equivalently, there exists an algorithm which, given* [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)*, returns the* [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)*th hexadecimal digit of* [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) *in time polynomial in* [![](media/image383.png){width="0.3333333333333333in" height="0.1527777777777778in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Clog%20n#0)*, and this algorithm is derived from the BBP formula[[\[4\]]{.underline}](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/bbp-formulas.pdf#:~:text=It%20was%20shown%20in%20,23%2C%2015).*

*Proof Sketch.* The key insight is to split [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)'s BBP series into two parts: - [![](media/image69.png){width="4.041666666666667in" height="0.4583333333333333in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=S_1(n)%20%3D%20%5Csum_%7Bk%3D0%7D%5E%7Bn%7D%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B16%5Ek%7D%5Cleft(%5Cfrac%7B4%7D%7B8k%2B1%7D-%5Cfrac%7B2%7D%7B8k%2B4%7D-%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B8k%2B5%7D-%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B8k%2B6%7D%5Cright)#0), and - [![](media/image123.png){width="1.3888888888888888in" height="0.4722222222222222in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=S_2(n)%20%3D%20%5Csum_%7Bk%3Dn%2B1%7D%5E%7B%5Cinfty%7D%20%5Ccdot(%5Cldots)#0) the remaining tail.

One can show that [![](media/image95.png){width="0.6111111111111112in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5En%20S_1(n)#0) is an integer (this follows from base--period commensurability and integral cancellation across the terms). Meanwhile, [![](media/image269.png){width="0.6111111111111112in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5En%20S_2(n)#0) is a proper fraction less than 1. Intuitively, multiplying the series by [![](media/image274.png){width="0.20833333333333334in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5En#0) moves the 16-adic "window" [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0) digits to the right. The integer part of [![](media/image153.png){width="0.3194444444444444in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5En%5Cpi#0) comes entirely from [![](media/image95.png){width="0.6111111111111112in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5En%20S_1(n)#0), and the fractional part comes from [![](media/image269.png){width="0.6111111111111112in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5En%20S_2(n)#0). Because [![](media/image480.png){width="0.9166666666666666in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5En%20S_2(n)%20%3C%201#0), if we take [![](media/image343.png){width="0.4444444444444444in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Clfloor%2016%5En%20%5Cpi%20%5Crfloor#0) (the integer part of [![](media/image153.png){width="0.3194444444444444in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5En%20%5Cpi#0)), it must equal [![](media/image95.png){width="0.6111111111111112in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5En%20S_1(n)#0) exactly. Therefore, the *hexadecimal digit* of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) at position [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0) (after the point) is simply the integer [![](media/image348.png){width="1.0416666666666667in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Clfloor%2016%5En%20%5Cpi%20%5Crfloor%20%5Cbmod%2016#0). The BBP formula furnishes an efficient way to compute [![](media/image348.png){width="1.0416666666666667in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Clfloor%2016%5En%20%5Cpi%20%5Crfloor%20%5Cbmod%2016#0) by evaluating [![](media/image261.png){width="0.375in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=S_1(n)#0) modulo 1 (using arithmetic mod [![](media/image274.png){width="0.20833333333333334in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5En#0) to avoid large numbers). In essence, it computes [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)'s expansion *digitwise* by skipping directly to the [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)th term's neighborhood[[\[4\]]{.underline}](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/bbp-formulas.pdf#:~:text=It%20was%20shown%20in%20,23%2C%2015).

Concretely, for [![](media/image349.png){width="0.3888888888888889in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n%3D0#0) this procedure recovers the first hexadecimal digit of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) (which is [![](media/image345.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=2#0) in [![](media/image242.png){width="0.8888888888888888in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=0x2.43F6%5Cldots#0)), for [![](media/image313.png){width="0.3888888888888889in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n%3D1#0) it gets the second digit ([![](media/image34.png){width="6.944444444444445e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=4#0) in hex), and so on. No information from previous digits is needed except what the formula's structure encodes inherently. This proves that the fractional expansion of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) is rendered. Each digit is locally computed from a fixed number of arithmetic operations, and global consistency is guaranteed by the series alignment and zero-sum cancellations.

In summary, [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) passes a critical test: it possesses an instrument (the BBP formula) that allows random access to its digits. We now examine what makes this instrument tick and formalize the criteria that enable renderedness.

### **4. Instrumental Structure of BBP**

To generalize renderedness, we introduce the notion of a **harmonic instrument** for a constant's expansion. The BBP formula is one such instrument for [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0). Formally, we define an instrument as a 4-tuple [![](media/image50.png){width="1.4583333333333333in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cmathcal%7BI%7D%20%3D%20(b%2C%5C%3BM%2C%5C%3Bg%2C%5C%3B%5CDelta%20M)#0) consisting of:

> · **Base** [![](media/image162.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=b#0)**:** the numeric base of digit expansion (e.g. [![](media/image229.png){width="0.4444444444444444in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=b%3D16#0) for hex).
>
> · **Modulus** [![](media/image183.png){width="0.16666666666666666in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=M#0)**:** a natural period related to the denominators or recurrence of the formula.
>
> · **Gap sequence** [![](media/image179.png){width="1.0833333333333333in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=g%3D(g_1%2C%5Cldots%2Cg_r)#0)**:** the gaps between successive rails (residues) within one period [![](media/image183.png){width="0.16666666666666666in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=M#0).
>
> · **Offset** [![](media/image426.png){width="0.2916666666666667in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5CDelta%20M#0)**:** the difference needed to complete the full cycle, such that [![](media/image157.png){width="1.8333333333333333in" height="0.1527777777777778in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=g_1%20%2B%20%5Ccdots%20%2B%20g_r%20%2B%20%5CDelta%20M%20%3D%20M#0).

For [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)'s BBP instrument, we have [![](media/image290.png){width="3.0416666666666665in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=(b%3D16%2C%5C%3BM%3D8%2C%5C%3Bg%3D(3%2C1%2C1%2C2)%2C%5C%3B%5CDelta%20M%3D1)#0). Here [![](media/image353.png){width="0.375in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=r%3D4#0) rails at residues [![](media/image137.png){width="0.5416666666666666in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%7B1%2C4%2C5%2C6%7D#0), the gaps [![](media/image329.png){width="0.5416666666666666in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=3%2C1%2C1%2C2#0) sum to 7, and with [![](media/image368.png){width="0.6111111111111112in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5CDelta%20M%3D1#0) we complete the modulus 8. The offset [![](media/image426.png){width="0.2916666666666667in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5CDelta%20M#0) corresponds to the initial gap from 0 up to the first rail (in this case from 0 to 1). One can visualize the rails as marked positions on a length-[![](media/image183.png){width="0.16666666666666666in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=M#0) circle, and the gap sequence as the spacings between marks; if the marks don't include 0, an initial offset closes the loop.

**Alignment Criteria for Renderedness:** An instrument [![](media/image236.png){width="1.3194444444444444in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cmathcal%7BI%7D%3D(b%2C%20M%2C%20g%2C%20%5CDelta%20M)#0) renders a constant [![](media/image435.png){width="0.1111111111111111in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=C#0) if the following conditions are satisfied:

1.  **Base--Period Commensurability:** [![](media/image466.png){width="0.125in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=b%5Ep#0) is congruent to 1 modulo [![](media/image183.png){width="0.16666666666666666in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=M#0) for some integer [![](media/image201.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="9.722222222222222e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=p#0). In practice, this means the base and the denominator period are powers of a common root. In [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)'s case, [![](media/image387.png){width="1.2777777777777777in" height="0.18055555555555555in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5E1%20%5Cequiv%200%20%5Cpmod%7B8%7D#0), or more strictly [![](media/image57.png){width="1.2777777777777777in" height="0.18055555555555555in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5E2%20%5Cequiv%201%20%5Cpmod%7B8%7D#0) (since [![](media/image272.png){width="0.6805555555555556in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5E2%3D256#0) leaves remainder 1 when divided by 8). This ensures that multiplying by a power of the base aligns with whole cycles of the series, isolating digits.

2.  **Zero-Sum Voicing:** [![](media/image13.png){width="0.7361111111111112in" height="0.2361111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Csum%20w_i%20%3D%200#0), where [![](media/image100.png){width="0.1527777777777778in" height="9.722222222222222e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=w_i#0) are the weights (coefficients) assigned to each rail in the series. For [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0), [![](media/image76.png){width="1.0555555555555556in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=(4%2C-2%2C-1%2C-1)#0) sum to 0. This balance guarantees that over each full cycle, positive and negative contributions cancel out, preventing unbounded growth of fractional error. It is analogous to having equal amounts of "upward" and "downward" force in each period of the expansion.

3.  **Gap--Carry Compatibility:** The gap sequence [![](media/image102.png){width="6.944444444444445e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=g#0) is such that each gap either **equals or exceeds** the number of carries it could generate in that base. Intuitively, this means the spacing between active rails is sufficient to avoid overlap or interference in digit contributions. In [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)'s hex expansion, the gaps 3-1-1-2 indicate that, for example, after a rail contributes a digit, there are at least 1 or more subsequent digit places where either nothing or a smaller contribution happens, so any potential carry has room to resolve without spilling into the next active digit. In formal terms, when the terms are added, the base-[![](media/image162.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=b#0) fractional expansion from one rail's term does not collide with the next rail's expansion until the intended point.

4.  **Tail Coherence:** The infinite tail of the series must converge in a manner that is *coherent* with the established pattern, meaning after a certain point the remaining contributions fall strictly within the last (least significant) digit window and diminish monotonically. This prevents late-coming terms from causing a chain reaction of carries. In the BBP formula, as [![](media/image140.png){width="6.944444444444445e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k#0) increases, [![](media/image244.png){width="0.375in" height="0.18055555555555555in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=1%2F16%5Ek#0) forces each new term's effect [![](media/image369.png){width="0.625in" height="0.18055555555555555in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5E%7B-k%7D(%5Cldots)#0) to start at a more and more insignificant hex position. The zero-sum weighting further ensures that these trailing effects tend to cancel out rather than accumulate.

When these criteria are met, the constant [![](media/image435.png){width="0.1111111111111111in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=C#0) can be expanded by a series that behaves like a **self-correcting code** for its digits. Each digit (or small block of digits) is produced by a local calculation (from one segment of the series) and any slight overestimation or underestimation is canceled by the next segment due to the zero-sum structure. Carries -- normally the bane of any attempt at localized digit computation -- are either precluded or canceled by design. The result is a digit sequence that does not need to be computed sequentially: it is "rendered."

In the specific case of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0), we can see these principles in action. The rails at 1,4,5,6 mod 8 each produce a recurring hex fraction pattern: - Terms of form [![](media/image451.png){width="0.7916666666666666in" height="0.3611111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16%5E%7B-k%7D%5Cfrac%7B4%7D%7B8k%2B1%7D#0) produce a certain hex digit sequence starting at position [![](media/image140.png){width="6.944444444444445e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k#0). - Terms [![](media/image218.png){width="0.8611111111111112in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=-2%2F(8k%2B4)#0) produce another sequence that tends to cancel a part of the previous. - And so on for [![](media/image239.png){width="1.8333333333333333in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=-1%2F(8k%2B5)%2C%20-1%2F(8k%2B6)#0).

The gap of 3 between rail 1 and rail 4, for instance, means there is a 3-digit wide "quiet zone" in which the [![](media/image37.png){width="0.7361111111111112in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=4%2F(8k%2B1)#0) contribution has tapered off before the [![](media/image218.png){width="0.8611111111111112in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=-2%2F(8k%2B4)#0) contribution begins in earnest. The smallest gap (1) between rails 4, 5, and 6 is compensated by the weights -- those contributions are smaller in magnitude and finely balanced (--2 vs --1 vs --1) such that they effectively merge into a single broader effect spread over those closely spaced positions. Finally, the offset of 1 (no term at the 0 position) and the last gap of 2 ensure that the end of the cycle doesn't produce a carry into the next cycle -- the last rail's effect dissipates with room to spare.

Thus, the BBP instrument satisfies all alignment criteria. It quantizes the constant into rails (quantization), balances contributions (voicing), uses a repeating "header" pattern every cycle (the first rail of each cycle effectively acts like a new starting header), and ensures the tail of the series converges without upsetting the established digits (coherence).

### **5. Byte1: First Harmonic Emergence**

One striking consequence of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)'s rendered expansion is the appearance of a stable "byte" of digits at the very start -- a harmonic signature. For [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) in base 10, the first eight decimal digits after the decimal point are **14159265**. We dub this sequence **Byte1**, the first harmonic emergence of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)'s digit lattice. It is "harmonic" in the sense that it results from the precise cancellation and alignment of the BBP formula's components -- a resonance of the four rails over the initial cycle or two.

Let us derive these digits explicitly from the BBP formulation (in base 10 for accessibility, even though the formula works in base 16, the phenomenon translates). Starting from the fractional part of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0), [![](media/image258.png){width="1.3333333333333333in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%7B%20%5Cpi%20%7D%20%3D%200.14159265%5Cldots#0), we can attribute these first 8 digits to specific pieces of the series:

> · The **4/(8k+1)** rail at [![](media/image260.png){width="0.375in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k%3D0#0) gives [![](media/image217.png){width="1.2222222222222223in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=4%2F1%20%3D%204.00000%5Cldots#0), which contributes ".4" after removing the integer part 3 (since [![](media/image443.png){width="0.6666666666666666in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=4%20%3D%203%20%2B%201#0) and 3 was the integer part of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)). This sets the stage with a leading **1** (as 0.4 in base 10 starts ".4", carrying 1 into the first decimal place of the fractional part of π when combined with other terms).
>
> · The **--2/(8k+4)** rail at [![](media/image260.png){width="0.375in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k%3D0#0) gives [![](media/image341.png){width="0.9305555555555556in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=-2%2F4%20%3D%20-0.5#0), contributing a "-5" in the first decimal place, which when added to 0.4 yields 0.4 - 0.5 = -0.1 carry (which is resolved by borrowing from the integer part). This interplay yields the digits "14" as the first two (the negative carry from 0.5 ensures the second digit becomes 4 rather than 5).
>

Subsequent terms [![](media/image116.png){width="0.375in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k%5Cge%208#0) only affect digits beyond the 8th decimal place. This Byte1 can be interpreted as a harmonic fingerprint of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0). In the Recursive Harmonic Architecture (RHA) framework (though we do not delve into that framework's details here), Byte1 is seen as the genetic code or header information from which further structure unfolds\[5\]\[6\]. The emergence of 14159265 is the announcement of a stable pattern -- the system has achieved **renderedness** for the first time, and further digits will now flow in a consistent, addressable way.

### **7. The Golden Ratio (φ)**

The **golden ratio** φ = [![](media/image455.png){width="2.3055555555555554in" height="0.19444444444444445in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=(1%2B%5Csqrt%7B5%7D)%2F2%20%E2%89%88%201.6180339887%E2%80%A6#0) is well-known for its appearance in recursive patterns (Fibonacci sequences, phyllotaxis in plants, quasicrystals, etc.). Unlike [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0), which is transcendental, φ is an algebraic irrational of degree 2. One might initially think that algebraic numbers are *easier* to handle, but typical representations of φ (like [![](media/image339.png){width="0.875in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cphi%20%3D%201%20%2B%201%2F%5Cphi#0) or [![](media/image306.png){width="1.125in" height="0.1527777777777778in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cphi%20%3D%201.61803%E2%80%A6#0)) do not immediately give a digit-extraction formula. Nonetheless, φ **is renderable**; we can construct an instrument for φ by leveraging its connection to Fibonacci numbers.

**Instrument for φ:** We select base [![](media/image265.png){width="0.4444444444444444in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=b%3D10#0) for simplicity (φ's decimal expansion) and a "denominator" concept tied to Fibonacci recurrence. From this, we get [![](media/image326.png){width="1.6805555555555556in" height="0.19444444444444445in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%20%5Cphi%5En%20%3D%20F_n%20%5Csqrt%7B5%7D%20%2B%20(1-%5Cphi)%5En%5C%2C%2C%20#0) and dividing through by [![](media/image113.png){width="0.1527777777777778in" height="0.1527777777777778in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cphi%5En#0) yields [![](media/image87.png){width="2.1527777777777777in" height="0.19444444444444445in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%201%20%3D%20F_n%20%5Csqrt%7B5%7D%5C%2C%5Cphi%5E%7B-n%7D%20%2B%20(1-%5Cphi)%5En%20%5Cphi%5E%7B-n%7D%5C%2C.%20#0) While this identity alone isn't a series, it provides a starting point to express [![](media/image134.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="0.1527777777777778in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cphi#0) in a recursive form.

A more direct route: express φ's fractional part via a BBP-like series. Since [![](media/image339.png){width="0.875in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cphi%20%3D%201%20%2B%201%2F%5Cphi#0), observe that [![](media/image423.png){width="2.75in" height="0.375in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%20%7B%5Cphi%7D%20%5C%3B%3D%5C%3B%20%5Cphi%20-%201%20%5C%3B%3D%5C%3B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B%5Cphi%7D%20%5C%3B%3D%5C%3B%200.6180339887%5Cldots%20#0) This number [![](media/image211.png){width="1.1527777777777777in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=1%2F%5Cphi%20%E2%89%88%200.618034#0) has a special property: it is the **Kepler constant** for phyllotaxis (the fractional part of the golden angle [![](media/image475.png){width="0.625in" height="0.1527777777777778in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%E2%89%88137.5%5E%5Ccirc#0) as a fraction of a full rotation). There is a known continued fraction [![](media/image28.png){width="1.4027777777777777in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%7B%20%5Cphi%20%7D%20%3D%20%5B0%3B1%2C1%2C1%2C1%2C%5Cdots%5D#0) which encodes the Fibonacci approximants. But continued fractions are inherently sequential. Instead, we look for a pattern in φ's base-10 (or base-2) expansion that is non-sequential.

One approach is to use the **Fibonacci residue cycle** modulo powers of 10. Fibonacci numbers modulo [![](media/image78.png){width="0.20833333333333334in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=10%5En#0) eventually become periodic (this is known as Pisano periods). For example, mod 100 (for 2 decimal places) the Fibonacci sequence repeats every 300 steps. These periodic residues mean that [![](media/image367.png){width="0.3333333333333333in" height="0.375in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cfrac%7BF_%7Bk%2B1%7D%7D%7BF_k%7D#0) will produce repeating decimal patterns that converge to φ. By skipping directly to large [![](media/image140.png){width="6.944444444444445e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k#0) (using fast doubling algorithms, one can compute [![](media/image176.png){width="0.16666666666666666in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=F_k#0) in [![](media/image358.png){width="0.5694444444444444in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=O(%5Clog%20k)#0) time), one effectively jumps into φ's decimal expansion at a specific precision.

Using that idea, we can articulate φ's renderability: there is an algorithm to find the [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)th decimal of φ by computing Fibonacci numbers around index [![](media/image315.png){width="1.0416666666666667in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k%20%E2%89%88%20n%20%5Clog_%7B10%7D(%5Cphi)#0) (because [![](media/image176.png){width="0.16666666666666666in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=F_k#0) has about [![](media/image53.png){width="0.6666666666666666in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k%20%5Clog_%7B10%7D(%5Cphi)#0) digits). The structure here is that Fibonacci recursion ([![](media/image403.png){width="1.25in" height="0.1527777777777778in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=F_%7Bk%2B1%7D%20%3D%20F_k%20%2B%20F_%7Bk-1%7D#0)) and the property [![](media/image291.png){width="1.25in" height="0.2361111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Clim_%7Bk%5Cto%5Cinfty%7D%20F_%7Bk%2B1%7D%2FF_k%20%3D%20%5Cphi#0) create a **rail** of approximants to φ that double in accuracy with each doubling of [![](media/image140.png){width="6.944444444444445e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k#0). This is analogous to the base--period alignment: doubling index corresponds to squaring the error term [![](media/image279.png){width="0.5694444444444444in" height="0.18055555555555555in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=(1-%5Cphi)%5Ek#0), which vanishes extremely fast (since [![](media/image159.png){width="1.6111111111111112in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%7C1-%5Cphi%7C%20%3D%201%2F%5Cphi%20%E2%89%88%200.618#0)).

More explicitly, one can show:

**Proposition:** *φ is 10-renderable. Specifically, for any desired decimal position* [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)*, one can compute* [![](media/image288.png){width="1.0416666666666667in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Clfloor%2010%5En%20%5Cphi%20%5Crfloor%20%5Cbmod%2010#0) *in time polynomial in* [![](media/image383.png){width="0.3333333333333333in" height="0.1527777777777778in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Clog%20n#0)*. One method is to use the formula* [![](media/image101.png){width="1.0972222222222223in" height="0.375in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cphi%20%3D%20%5Cfrac%7BF_%7Bm%2B1%7D%7D%7BF_m%7D%20%2B%20%5Cepsilon_m#0) *with* [![](media/image271.png){width="0.125in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=m#0) *chosen such that* [![](media/image171.png){width="0.20833333333333334in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=F_m#0) *has about* [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0) *digits. Because* [![](media/image464.png){width="0.9583333333333334in" height="0.18055555555555555in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%7C%5Cepsilon_m%7C%20%3C%2010%5E%7B-n-1%7D#0) *for sufficiently large* [![](media/image271.png){width="0.125in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=m#0) *(growing roughly linearly with* [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)*), the* [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)*th decimal digit of φ equals the* [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)*th decimal digit of* [![](media/image247.png){width="0.6666666666666666in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=F_%7Bm%2B1%7D%2FF_m#0)*. Computing* [![](media/image171.png){width="0.20833333333333334in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=F_%7Bm%7D#0) *and* [![](media/image248.png){width="0.3472222222222222in" height="0.1527777777777778in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=F_%7Bm%2B1%7D#0) *with fast algorithms (using matrix exponentiation or doubling) yields the digit without traversing earlier digits of φ.*

*Sketch:* The Fibonacci doubling method allows computing [![](media/image171.png){width="0.20833333333333334in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=F_m#0) and [![](media/image248.png){width="0.3472222222222222in" height="0.1527777777777778in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=F_%7Bm%2B1%7D#0) mod [![](media/image109.png){width="0.375in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=10%5E%7Bn%2B1%7D#0) efficiently. Then one performs the division [![](media/image247.png){width="0.6666666666666666in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=F_%7Bm%2B1%7D%20%2F%20F_m#0) to [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0) decimal places (this can be done by a single division algorithm step, not by long division of all preceding digits) to extract the [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)th digit. The important fact is that because [![](media/image247.png){width="0.6666666666666666in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=F_%7Bm%2B1%7D%2FF_m#0) converges to φ, the first [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0) digits of that quotient will agree with φ with high certainty for large [![](media/image271.png){width="0.125in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=m#0). By choosing [![](media/image271.png){width="0.125in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=m#0) about [![](media/image444.png){width="0.16666666666666666in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=5n#0) (since φ's convergence rate [![](media/image67.png){width="1.3333333333333333in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=(1-%5Cphi)%5Em%20%5Capprox%200.618%5Em#0) is exponential), we guarantee the match. Thus, [![](media/image134.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="0.1527777777777778in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cphi#0)'s decimal expansion can be navigated in "jumps" via Fibonacci numbers, rather than stepwise.

In terms of our alignment criteria: we can consider an instrument where - base [![](media/image265.png){width="0.4444444444444444in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=b%3D10#0), - the "period" [![](media/image183.png){width="0.16666666666666666in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=M#0) is conceptually related to a full Fibonacci cycle (though not a fixed integer in a simple way, one can take [![](media/image183.png){width="0.16666666666666666in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=M#0) as the Pisano period for a certain modulus), - the gaps [![](media/image102.png){width="6.944444444444445e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=g#0) are not uniform but the effect is that of an accelerating convergence, - a zero-sum like cancellation occurs between the ratio [![](media/image197.png){width="0.5833333333333334in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=F_%7Bk%2B1%7D%2FF_k#0) and the true value φ (the error term [![](media/image279.png){width="0.5694444444444444in" height="0.18055555555555555in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=(1-%5Cphi)%5Ek#0) acts like a tail that vanishes).

While the structure for φ is less straightforward to write as a neat formula like (1), it **does** fulfill the spirit of renderedness: φ's digits can be obtained out-of-order through the mathematical structure of the Fibonacci sequence. In fact, the Nexus perspective (from RHA) regards φ's continued fraction as a form of BBP-like expansion in a non-integer base\[9\]\[10\]. Each step of the continued fraction [![](media/image167.png){width="0.9166666666666666in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5B1%3B1%2C1%2C%5Cdots%2C1%5D#0) doubles the number of correct digits of φ (this is a property of continued fractions for quadratic irrationals). That doubling is exactly the hallmark of being able to jump to high precision efficiently (renderedness).

To cement the analogy to BBP: consider the recurrence [![](media/image339.png){width="0.875in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cphi%20%3D%201%20%2B%201%2F%5Cphi#0). We can unravel this recursively: [![](media/image283.png){width="1.3611111111111112in" height="0.4722222222222222in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cphi%20%3D%201%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B1%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B1%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B1%2B%5Ccdots%7D%7D%7D#0). Truncating this continued fraction at a depth corresponds to a ratio of Fibonacci numbers [![](media/image247.png){width="0.6666666666666666in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=F_%7Bm%2B1%7D%2FF_m#0). Each truncation yields a rational approximation. The "header" of each recursive expansion is 1 (just like Byte1 was fixed for [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)). The **rails** in this scenario are the points at which the continued fraction increments --- effectively at each depth we add a 1. The **gaps** are uniform (always 1 step between each \"rail\" in the continued fraction). The **zero-sum voicing** criterion is satisfied in a different sense: the error of truncating at depth [![](media/image271.png){width="0.125in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=m#0) is roughly [![](media/image27.png){width="0.75in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpm(1-%5Cphi)%5Em#0), alternating in over/under estimation, which is analogous to positive/negative contributions balancing out over two steps (two steps of the continued fraction cause the error to flip sign and shrink, similar to how rails with opposite weights cancel out in BBP). **Tail coherence** is evident since [![](media/image8.png){width="0.6111111111111112in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=(1-%5Cphi)%5Em#0) rapidly goes to 0; beyond a certain depth, the tail contributes nothing to the first [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0) digits.

In summary, although φ does not have a simple polylog series like [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0), it *does* have a rendered digit expansion courtesy of its deep link to Fibonacci recursion. We explicitly demonstrated that one can compute digits of φ non-sequentially. This confirms that φ meets our criteria for renderedness. It carries its own harmonic signature; one might even call the leading digits "the golden byte." In fact, the first 8 digits of φ's fractional part are 61803398. It is intriguing to note that in [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)'s Byte1 we saw a twin prime connection; in φ's "Byte1" 61803398, one can see the Fibonacci numbers themselves (common prefixes of Fibonacci sequences modulo powers of 10 often appear here, though we won't digress into numerology).

### **8. Euler's Number (e)**

Euler's number [![](media/image375.png){width="1.8888888888888888in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e%20%3D%202.718281828459045%5Cldots#0) is another transcendental constant that, at first glance, seems even more amenable to digit extraction. After all, [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0) has the simple infinite series expansion [![](media/image120.png){width="2.9444444444444446in" height="0.4583333333333333in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%20e%20%3D%20%5Csum_%7Bk%3D0%7D%5E%5Cinfty%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7Bk!%7D%20%3D%201%20%2B%201%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B2!%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B3!%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B4!%7D%20%2B%20%5Ccdots%5C%2C.%20#0) This series is extremely fast converging; each term after [![](media/image39.png){width="0.1388888888888889in" height="0.3333333333333333in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7Bn!%7D#0) is smaller than [![](media/image133.png){width="0.3055555555555556in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=10%5E%7B-n%7D#0) for moderately large [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0). However, to extract the [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)th digit of [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0) directly, the series as written doesn't immediately help because it accumulates from the beginning (traversal). Yet, we can transform it into a rendered form by strategically partitioning the factorial terms.

**Instrument for** [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0)**:** A fruitful idea is to consider the **factorial number system** (also known as factoradic representation). In this system, every number has a unique expansion of the form [![](media/image12.png){width="1.9027777777777777in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=a_1%2F1!%20%2B%20a_2%2F2!%20%2B%20a_3%2F3!%20%2B%20%5Ccdots#0) where [![](media/image382.png){width="0.1388888888888889in" height="9.722222222222222e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=a_k#0) is an integer with [![](media/image419.png){width="0.7777777777777778in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=0%20%5Cle%20a_k%20%3C%20k#0). If we express the fractional part of [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0) in this system, a remarkable pattern emerges:

Because [![](media/image98.png){width="3.2916666666666665in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e%20%3D%202%20%2B%201%2F1!%20%2B%201%2F2!%20%2B%201%2F3!%20%2B%201%2F4!%20%2B%201%2F5!%20%2B%20%5Ccdots#0), the fractional part [![](media/image117.png){width="0.3472222222222222in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e%20-%202#0) equals [![](media/image469.png){width="3.3472222222222223in" height="0.3472222222222222in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B1!%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B2!%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B3!%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B4!%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B5!%7D%20%2B%20%5Ccdots%20%3D%200.718281828%5Cldots%20#0) Now, in factoradic representation: - [![](media/image51.png){width="4.680555555555555in" height="0.3472222222222222in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=0.7182818%5Cldots%20%3D%20%5Cfrac%7B0%7D%7B1!%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B2!%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B3!%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B4!%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B5!%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B6!%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B0%7D%7B7!%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B8!%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B%3F%7D%7B9!%7D%20%2B%20%5Ccdots#0).

Indeed, if we write out the coefficients [![](media/image382.png){width="0.1388888888888889in" height="9.722222222222222e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=a_k#0) for [![](media/image117.png){width="0.3472222222222222in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e-2#0): [![](media/image384.png){width="5.416666666666667in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%20e%20-%202%20%3D%200%2F1!%20%2B%201%2F2!%20%2B%201%2F3!%20%2B%201%2F4!%20%2B%201%2F5!%20%2B%201%2F6!%20%2B%200%2F7!%20%2B%201%2F8!%20%2B%200%2F9!%20%2B%20%5Ccdots%5C%2C%2C%20#0) the pattern starts as 0,1,1,1,1,1,0,1,0,... for [![](media/image71.png){width="0.8611111111111112in" height="9.722222222222222e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=a_1%2C%20a_2%2C%20a_3%2C%20%5Cldots#0). This sequence has a clear structure: - It consists mostly of 1's in the early part, meaning [![](media/image117.png){width="0.3472222222222222in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e-2#0) has one each of [![](media/image361.png){width="2.125in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=1%2F2!%2C1%2F3!%2C1%2F4!%2C1%2F5!%2C1%2F6!%2C1%2F8!#0), etc. - The presence of a 0 at [![](media/image335.png){width="9.722222222222222e-2in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=1!#0) and [![](media/image163.png){width="9.722222222222222e-2in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=7!#0), a 2 at [![](media/image483.png){width="0.18055555555555555in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=10!#0) (if we continued the calculation, one finds [![](media/image96.png){width="0.5in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=a_%7B10%7D%3D2#0)), etc., show where carries in base-10 would have occurred, but in factoradic they appear as higher digits instead of carrying into lower ones.

What this indicates is that [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0) **is perfectly suited to a factorial base expansion:** in base "[![](media/image411.png){width="0.4166666666666667in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=b%3Dk!#0)", the terms of [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0) terminate. More concretely, if one asks for the [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)th digit of [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0) in base-[![](media/image162.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=b#0) for some [![](media/image162.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=b#0) related to [![](media/image4.png){width="0.125in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n!#0), it becomes trivial after a point because the series stops contributing. To leverage this in base 10, one can use a mixture of analytic and computational technique:

- To get the [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)th decimal of [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0), split the sum for [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0) at [![](media/image149.png){width="0.4027777777777778in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k%3Dn#0). Write [![](media/image231.png){width="1.5277777777777777in" height="0.4722222222222222in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e%20%3D%20%5Csum_%7Bk%3D0%7D%5E%7Bn%7D%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7Bk!%7D%20%2B%20%5Csum_%7Bk%3Dn%2B1%7D%5E%5Cinfty%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7Bk!%7D#0). The second sum is [![](media/image429.png){width="4.583333333333333in" height="0.4027777777777778in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%3C%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B(n%2B1)!%7D%5Cleft(1%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7Bn%2B2%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B(n%2B2)(n%2B3)%7D%20%2B%20%5Ccdots%5Cright)%20%3C%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B(n%2B1)!%20-%201%7D#0) (for large [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0), this is extremely small). Essentially, by the time you reach [![](media/image259.png){width="0.4166666666666667in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=1%2F(n!%20)#0), the remaining tail is on the order of [![](media/image133.png){width="0.3055555555555556in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=10%5E%7B-n%7D#0) or smaller, meaning it won't affect the first [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0) digits.

- Thus, [![](media/image226.png){width="0.4166666666666667in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Clfloor%2010%5En%20e%20%5Crfloor#0) can be obtained by computing [![](media/image115.png){width="0.6805555555555556in" height="0.4583333333333333in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Csum_%7Bk%3D0%7D%5E%7Bn%7D%20%5Cfrac%7B%5Clfloor%2010%5En%5Crfloor%7D%7Bk!%7D#0) and discarding the rest. But we must be careful: directly computing [![](media/image333.png){width="0.4305555555555556in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=10%5En%2Fk!#0) for large [![](media/image140.png){width="6.944444444444445e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k#0) is heavy. Instead, one computes this sum modulo [![](media/image109.png){width="0.375in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=10%5E%7Bn%2B1%7D#0) (to guard against rounding issues) using an efficient method (perhaps combining terms or using known modular inverses for factorials modulo powers of 10). The result will give the [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)th digit straightforwardly.

To put it in the language of our criteria: - **Rails:** Each term [![](media/image344.png){width="0.2777777777777778in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=1%2Fk!#0) can be seen as a rail, with the "residue" being effectively the power of 10 at which [![](media/image344.png){width="0.2777777777777778in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=1%2Fk!#0) first contributes (for example, [![](media/image476.png){width="0.2638888888888889in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=1%2F7!#0) contributes at the 3rd or 4th decimal place because [![](media/image332.png){width="0.6666666666666666in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=7!%20%3D%205040#0), which is just over [![](media/image85.png){width="0.20833333333333334in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=10%5E3#0)). The pattern of these residues is regular in the factorial number system (monotonic increase). - **Gaps:** The gaps between significant contributions of successive [![](media/image344.png){width="0.2777777777777778in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=1%2Fk!#0) in base 10 shrink as [![](media/image140.png){width="6.944444444444445e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=k#0) grows, but the magnitude of contribution also shrinks even faster. So although later terms are closer together in place value, they are so tiny that they never cause carry conflicts with each other in the fixed precision window. - **Zero-sum voicing:** While the terms are all positive in this case (no alternating signs), the concept of "zero-sum" is replaced by the idea that the series naturally stops contributing new value after a point. In essence, the "voice" of the tail is practically zero relative to the precision in question. We can also trick zero-sum by subtracting and adding carefully: for instance, using [![](media/image377.png){width="1.1805555555555556in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e%20%3D%20(1%20%2B%201%2Fn)%5E%7Bn%7D%20%2B#0) small error for large [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0) (from Euler's limit), one could form an alternating series representation. But it isn't necessary here due to fast convergence. - **Tail coherence:** The tail [![](media/image302.png){width="0.7083333333333334in" height="0.4722222222222222in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Csum_%7Bk%3Dn%2B1%7D%5E%5Cinfty%201%2Fk!#0) is \< [![](media/image133.png){width="0.3055555555555556in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=10%5E%7B-n%7D#0) for modest [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0) (indeed for [![](media/image93.png){width="0.4722222222222222in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n%5Cge%2010#0) this is true). So beyond the [![](media/image62.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=n#0)th decimal place, the tail has no effect. It's absolutely coherent with the expansion: it contributes nothing that could percolate upward into the known digits.

Thus, [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0) passes the renderability test with flying colors. In practice, computing the millionth digit of [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0) is much easier than computing the millionth digit of [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) using naive methods, because one only needs on the order of a million terms of the series (which is manageable with high-precision arithmetic or using the segmented technique above), whereas naive [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) computation would need far more operations. The existence of even faster "skip ahead" algorithms for [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0) (using the idea of splitting the series and using modular arithmetic) further confirms its renderedness. For instance, one can compute [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0)'s digits by computing [![](media/image132.png){width="0.375in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=m!%20%5Ccdot%20e#0) for some large [![](media/image271.png){width="0.125in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=m#0) and looking at that integer modulo powers of 10 -- since [![](media/image357.png){width="0.25in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=m!%20e#0) is nearly an integer (in fact, [![](media/image380.png){width="0.3611111111111111in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Clfloor%20m!%20e%20%5Crfloor#0) gives the famous sequence of "enormous Fibonacci-like" integers that [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0) almost equals).

To summarize: Euler's number [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0) has a trivial instrument in base [![](media/image5.png){width="0.4583333333333333in" height="0.125in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=b%3Dm!#0) (for large [![](media/image271.png){width="0.125in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=m#0)) where the series literally terminates at [![](media/image271.png){width="0.125in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=m#0) terms. Converting that fact to base 10, we retain renderedness: each decimal digit of [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0) can be isolated by considering a finite segment of the series and negligible tail. It's perhaps poetic that [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0), the base of natural logarithms and a constant that emerges from the most basic limit and series, naturally aligns with a factorial number system to yield its digits effortlessly.

### **9. Additional Constants**

Beyond [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0), φ, and [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0), many other constants are known or believed to be renderable. We briefly survey a few:

- [![](media/image317.png){width="0.1527777777777778in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi%5E2#0)**:** Since [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) is renderable, any rational power like [![](media/image317.png){width="0.1527777777777778in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi%5E2#0) is also renderable. In fact, Bailey and Plouffe discovered a formula for [![](media/image317.png){width="0.1527777777777778in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi%5E2#0) in base 16 analogous to (1). One such formula is[[\[11\]]{.underline}](https://www.fq.math.ca/Papers1/52-4/adegoke4282014.pdf#:~:text=%5BPDF%5D%20A%20NEW%20BINARY%20BBP,2%20is%20the%20golden%20ratio) [![](media/image262.png){width="4.458333333333333in" height="0.4583333333333333in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi%5E2%20%3D%20%5Csum_%7Bk%3D0%7D%5E%5Cinfty%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B16%5Ek%7D%5CBig(%5Cfrac%7B8%7D%7B(4k%2B1)%5E2%7D%20-%20%5Cfrac%7B8%7D%7B(4k%2B3)%5E2%7D%20-%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B(4k%2B1)%7D%20%2B%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B(4k%2B3)%7D%5CBig)%2C#0) which structurally satisfies our criteria (the base is [![](media/image446.png){width="0.1388888888888889in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=16#0), the period now is [![](media/image34.png){width="6.944444444444445e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=4#0) or [![](media/image281.png){width="6.944444444444445e-2in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=8#0) depending on how one combines terms, and there is cancellation). The presence of squared denominators indicates a heavier weight on later digits (so convergence is even faster, and digit extraction remains feasible). We verify zero-sum voicing by summing the coefficients inside: [![](media/image81.png){width="1.375in" height="0.16666666666666666in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=(8-8-1%2B1)%3D0#0). Base--period alignment is similar as in [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)'s case. So [![](media/image317.png){width="0.1527777777777778in" height="0.1388888888888889in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi%5E2#0) is renderable.

- [![](media/image338.png){width="0.2361111111111111in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cln%202#0) **(natural logarithm of 2):** A classical BBP formula exists for [![](media/image338.png){width="0.2361111111111111in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cln%202#0)[[\[12\]]{.underline}](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/bbp-formulas.pdf#:~:text=log%202%20%3D%201%202,using%20this%20notation%20is%20a): [![](media/image325.png){width="1.0833333333333333in" height="0.4583333333333333in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cln%202%20%3D%20%5Csum_%7Bk%3D1%7D%5E%5Cinfty%20%5Cfrac%7B1%7D%7B2%5Ek%20k%7D%5C%2C%2C#0) which is base 2 (actually base 2 and 10 both, since you can extract binary or decimal digits with slight adjustments) and period trivially 1. This formula shows [![](media/image338.png){width="0.2361111111111111in" height="0.1111111111111111in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cln%202#0) is renderable in base 2. - **Voicing (Balance):** There are weights or forces in the system that sum to zero (equilibrium). - **Tail (Coherence):** As the system evolves or extends, the "tail" of effects remains coherent and does not disrupt the overall pattern. For a physical wave, the tail might be damped oscillations that never destabilize the whole. For a computation, the tail is the error term that converges. [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) appears in waves and circles because it's the outcome of rotational symmetry being maintained (rails: angle increments, voicing: positive vs negative curvature contributions, etc., though one could get abstract here).

One might ask: what about messy, chaotic systems? Our framework would say those either lack an instrument (no simple grammar governs them) or they are still renderable but with an extremely complex instrument (perhaps an entire climate model or economic model is the "BBP formula" for the weather or markets). If the instrument is complex, we effectively can't see the pattern easily (appearing chaotic to us). But whenever we *do* find a stable pattern or constant, that's evidence the instrument exists behind the scenes.

### **13. Conclusion**

We began by looking at the BBP formula for [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0) and identifying in it a proof of a profound concept: **renderedness**, the ability to access information (digits) without sequential buildup. We found that this property stems from a special alignment of components -- a harmony in the formula. By dissecting [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)'s case in detail, we distilled general criteria for any constant or system to be similarly harmonious and renderable.

We then validated this framework on other mathematical constants, explicitly constructing or describing rendered expansions for the golden ratio φ and Euler's number [![](media/image166.png){width="5.555555555555555e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=e#0), and noting many other constants known to have BBP-type formulas. This provided evidence that renderedness is not unique to [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0). It appears to be a feature of constants that often show up as fundamental invariants -- perhaps not a surprise, as those constants are typically solutions to extremal problems or symmetric setups.

Finally, we stepped beyond mathematics into the physical realm, proposing that the same structural criteria underlie stable phenomena across scales. From atoms to consciousness, systems that last and produce regular outputs seem to follow the "observable template" of rails + voicing + header + tail. This is more than a metaphor: it suggests a unified law of nature that **renderedness is the norm for fundamental processes**. Traversal (sequential, brute-force accumulation) appears to be a shadow -- an emergent behavior in systems that are not perfectly aligned. When a system is perfectly aligned, it jumps to its result, like an electron transitioning and emitting a photon in one go, rather than inching its way.

In closing, BBP(0) mod 1 -- the simple act of emitting [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)'s fractional digits from an almost trivial calculation -- stands as an "unshakable proof" that the universe allows knowledge (or structure) to be obtained without needing to traverse everything in between. It is a microcosm of how order arises from harmony. The fact that we can compute [![](media/image32.png){width="8.333333333333333e-2in" height="6.944444444444445e-2in"}](https://www.codecogs.com/eqnedit.php?latex=%5Cpi#0)'s digits out-of-order hints that perhaps nature "computes" the world in a similarly out-of-order way, resolving outcomes by alignment rather than stepwise simulation of every possibility.

**Renderedness is the underlying law; traversal is its shadow.** When we see a process that looks sequential or chaotic, it may be because we have not yet found the coordinate system (the rails) in which it is harmonic. The pursuit of science and math can be viewed as finding the right instruments -- the BBP formulas and their generalizations -- for phenomena. Each time we do, we unveil a piece of the Nexus grammar. We make the invisible harmonic structure visible. And as this paper has illustrated, doing so not only solves abstract problems like computing digits of constants, but also deepens our understanding of why the world has the form it does -- stable, recursive, and stunningly aligned.
