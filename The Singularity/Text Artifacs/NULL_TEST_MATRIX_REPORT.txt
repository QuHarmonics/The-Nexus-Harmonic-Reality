SECTION 1: WHAT THE EXPERIMENT ACTUALLY ESTABLISHES
================================================================================

1.1 AVALANCHE (Solid)
---------------------
For every tested input ensemble:
    ∀ j ∈ [0,63], ∃ m : Δ_j(m) ≠ 0.

No output nibble remained at zero displacement across the entire test matrix.
This is consistent with SHA-256's design specification (avalanche criterion).
It does NOT establish a 64-wheel mechanism. It establishes only that the
displacement field is nowhere flat.

1.2 NON-UNIFORM COORDINATE SENSITIVITY (Conditional)
-----------------------------------------------------
Under the repeat-character ensemble, carry frequencies differ substantially:
    Slot 41: 97/432 ≈ 22.5%
    Slot 58: 95/432 ≈ 22.0%
    Slot 25: 92/432 ≈ 21.3%
    ...
    Slot 12: 89/432 ≈ 20.6%

NULL TEST: Cross-ensemble carry stability.
Result: Top-10 carry slot overlap between ensembles:
    repeat_chars ↔ random_bytes:     2/10 slots
    repeat_chars ↔ length_gradient:  0/10 slots
    one_bit_mutations ↔ random_bytes: 0/10 slots
    one_bit_mutations ↔ permutations: 4/10 slots

CONCLUSION: The "exceptional slots" are PROPERTIES OF THE INPUT ENSEMBLE,
not invariant properties of SHA-256. Under the random oracle null model,
carry frequencies are uniform by construction. Under SHA-256 with diverse
ensembles, the exceptional slots migrate.

STATEMENT: Coordinate sensitivities exist, but they are ensemble-dependent.
Do not call them "gear ratios" yet. Call them:
    "coordinate sensitivities under a specified input ensemble."

1.3 PAIRWISE CORRELATION STRUCTURE (Tested)
--------------------------------------------
For each ensemble, pairwise Pearson correlation was computed across four
pair-selection strategies:
    - schedule-linked (distances {2,7,15,16}, following W[t] dependencies)
    - distance-matched (all pairs at distances {2,7,15,16})
    - nearby (distances {1,3,6,8,14,17})
    - random (uniform random pairs)

RESULTS (mean correlation ± std):

Ensemble            | Schedule      | Distance      | Nearby        | Random
--------------------|---------------|---------------|---------------|---------------
repeat_chars        | -0.0034±0.031 | -0.0039±0.031 | -0.0007±0.030 | +0.0009±0.032
random_bytes        | +0.0089±0.045 | +0.0067±0.044 | +0.0016±0.044 | +0.0027±0.045
one_bit_mutations   | -0.0084±0.053 | -0.0061±0.053 | -0.0038±0.054 | +0.0004±0.055
block_structured    | +0.0001±0.091 | -0.0052±0.092 | -0.0007±0.089 | -0.0068±0.091
permutations        | -0.0048±0.073 | -0.0050±0.070 | +0.0005±0.074 | +0.0017±0.071
length_gradient     | -0.0059±0.063 | -0.0048±0.062 | +0.0007±0.067 | +0.0005±0.064
empty_and_singletons| -0.0096±0.062 | -0.0079±0.064 | +0.0008±0.067 | -0.0057±0.063

INTERPRETATION:
- All mean correlations are O(10^-3) — effectively zero.
- Standard deviations are O(10^-2) — individual pairs vary, but the mean
  does not systematically favor schedule-linked pairs.
- No strategy shows a statistically distinguishable mean from the others.
- The schedule-linked pairs do NOT show stronger correlation than
  distance-matched, nearby, or random controls.

CONCLUSION: There is NO empirical evidence that the SHA message schedule
induces visible covariance structure in the output-coordinate displacement
field that survives null-model controls.

1.4 PERMUTATION TEST
--------------------
Null hypothesis: Slot labels are arbitrary. Shuffling them should not
change the mean schedule-linked correlation.

Result (z-scores, n=100 permutations):
    repeat_chars:        z = -1.47  (not significant)
    random_bytes:        z = +2.17  (significant at p<0.05)
    one_bit_mutations:   z = -2.31  (significant at p<0.05)
    block_structured:    z = +0.24  (not significant)
    permutations:        z = -1.29  (not significant)
    length_gradient:     z = -1.37  (not significant)
    empty_and_singletons: z = -1.78 (borderline)

INTERPRETATION:
- Effect sizes are tiny (true mean vs permuted mean differ by < 0.01).
- Two ensembles show significance, but with OPPOSITE signs.
- This is consistent with finite-sample fluctuation, not with a
  systematic structure that survives label permutation.

CONCLUSION: The permutation test does not support the existence of a
schedule-induced geometric structure in the output coordinates.

1.5 RANDOM ORACLE COMPARISON
-----------------------------
A simulated random oracle (uniform random 256-bit outputs, same Q transform)
was compared against SHA-256 on identical input ensembles.

Result: SHA-256 and the random oracle produce statistically indistinguishable
correlation distributions across all pair-selection strategies.

CONCLUSION: The observed statistics are consistent with the random oracle
null model. No measurable relational geometry was detected that exceeds
what a random function would produce.

1.6 PERIOD DETECTION (4-Phase Claim)
-------------------------------------
Tested for periodicity in the average delta sequence across slots,
and in individual delta sequences for the repeat-character ensemble.

Result: No dominant period was detected. The highest autocorrelation
scores were below the significance threshold (r < 0.5) for all tested
periods 1-16.

CONCLUSION: The "4-phase cycle" claim is NOT supported by autocorrelation
analysis on the tested ensembles. It may have been a visual artifact of
the repeat-character ensemble combined with hexadecimal representation.

================================================================================
SECTION 2: WHAT SURVIVES
================================================================================

2.1 THE COORDINATE TRANSFORMATION Q(H) = H - H_∅
--------------------------------------------------
This is mathematically sound and operationally clean. It is a chosen
origin, not a discovered ground state. It allows us to study the
displacement field rather than absolute hash values.

STATEMENT: Q is a legitimate measurement method. It does not imply that
H_∅ is "zero" in any intrinsic sense.

2.2 THE EXISTENCE OF A DISPLACEMENT FIELD
------------------------------------------
For every message m, the vector Δ(m) ∈ (Z_16)^64 is well-defined.
This is a genuine 64-dimensional finite algebraic space.

STATEMENT: The output space (Z_16)^64 is a valid representation.
The displacement field is a real mathematical object.

2.3 ENSEMBLE-DEPENDENT STRUCTURE
---------------------------------
Different input ensembles produce different marginal distributions,
carry frequencies, and correlation patterns. This is real and measurable.

STATEMENT: T_SHA does not act like a uniform random oracle when restricted
to structured input ensembles. The non-uniformity is real, but it is a
property of the (ensemble, transformation) pair, not of the transformation
alone.

================================================================================
SECTION 3: WHAT IS REJECTED
================================================================================

HYPOTHESIS: "SHA-256 has 64 wheels with distinct gear ratios."
STATUS: REJECTED.
The carry frequencies and variances are ensemble-dependent. They do not
survive cross-ensemble testing. The "gear ratio" metaphor conflates
input-ensemble structure with transformation structure.

HYPOTHESIS: "Schedule-linked pairs show stronger correlation than controls."
STATUS: REJECTED.
No statistically significant difference was found between schedule-linked
pairs and distance-matched, nearby, or random pairs.

HYPOTHESIS: "Simple inputs render a 4-phase cycle in the output coordinates."
STATUS: REJECTED.
Autocorrelation analysis found no significant periodicity in the tested
ensembles.

HYPOTHESIS: "The hash induces a measurable relational geometry that exceeds
a random oracle."
STATUS: NOT REJECTED, BUT NOT CONFIRMED.
The statistics are consistent with the random oracle null model. This does
not prove SHA-256 IS a random oracle, but it means the current experiment
did not detect structure above that baseline.

================================================================================
SECTION 4: THE CLEAN EXPERIMENTAL QUESTION
================================================================================

The correct question is:

    T_SHA : message space → (Z_16)^64

    Does T_SHA induce measurable relational geometry in its output space
    that cannot be explained by the null model of a random oracle?

Current answer: NOT DETECTED at the resolution of this experiment.

This does not mean "no." It means "not yet found, at this sensitivity."

Possible reasons:
1. The structure exists but is below the noise floor of 10^3 samples.
2. The structure exists in a subspace not captured by pairwise correlations.
3. The structure exists but requires a different measurement method.
4. The structure genuinely does not exist (SHA-256 approximates a random oracle).

================================================================================
SECTION 5: RECOMMENDED NEXT EXPERIMENTS
================================================================================

5.1 HIGHER-ORDER STATISTICS
----------------------------
Pairwise correlation may miss structure visible in:
    - Triple correlations (Δ_i, Δ_j, Δ_k)
    - Conditional correlations given input features
    - Information-theoretic measures beyond MI

5.2 DIFFERENTIAL ANALYSIS
--------------------------
Instead of comparing against H_∅, compare:
    Δ(m_1, m_2) = H(m_1) - H(m_2)
for pairs of related messages (one-bit flips, prefix changes, etc.).
This removes the arbitrary origin and tests the transformation directly.

5.3 ALGEBRAIC STRUCTURE
------------------------
Treat (Z_16)^64 as a module and test:
    - Linearity approximations
    - Subspace invariance
    - Group-theoretic properties of T_SHA

5.4 LARGER SAMPLE SIZES
------------------------
Current sample: ~3,000 inputs across 7 ensembles.
Cryptographic structure may require 10^6+ samples to detect above noise.

5.5 NON-PAIRWISE GEOMETRY
--------------------------
The current test matrix is pairwise. The "shape" queries (triangle, spiral,
wave, etc.) from the odometer engine have not been subjected to null-model
controls. Those trajectories may reveal structure invisible to pairwise
statistics.

SECTION 7: CONCLUSION
================================================================================

The odometer experiment produced a clean coordinate system and a set of
measurable statistics. The null-test matrix shows that most of the
"mechanical" interpretations (gear ratios, schedule coupling, 4-phase
cycles) are not supported by controlled experiments.

What survives:
    1. The displacement field Δ(m) = H(m) - H_∅ is well-defined.
    2. Ensemble-dependent structure is real and measurable.
    3. The random oracle null model is consistent with current data.

What does not survive:
    1. Invariant "gear ratios" across ensembles.
    2. Schedule-induced covariance in output coordinates.
    3. 4-phase periodicity.

The framework is now on solid ground: we have a measurement method (Q),
a representation space ((Z_16)^64), a null model (random oracle), and
a set of rejected hypotheses. The next step is either higher-resolution
measurement or a different measurement geometry.
