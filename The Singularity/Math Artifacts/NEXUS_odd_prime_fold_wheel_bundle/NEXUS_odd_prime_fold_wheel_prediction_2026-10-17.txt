NEXUS prediction seal: odd-prime fold wheel classification
Date: 2026-10-17
Question: Under N(w)=1/p for prime p and finite rational nesting w^k in Q, does an odd-prime fold wheel exist in Q(sqrt(-d))? Separate the trivial half-turn from a nontrivial root-of-unity phase wheel.
Prediction before run:
1. A trivial solution exists for every prime p in Q(sqrt(-p)): w=sqrt(-p)/p, N(w)=1/p, w^2=-1/p. Therefore the literal condition w^k in Q does not rule out odd primes.
2. A nontrivial phase wheel whose phase is pi/(2p) requires a primitive 2p-th root of unity in an imaginary quadratic field. Its cyclotomic degree phi(2p)=p-1 must be <=2; hence only p=2 or p=3 can occur. Exact candidates are (1+i)/2 for p=2 and (3+sqrt(-3))/6 for p=3.
3. For p>3, the only nested solution in an arbitrary imaginary quadratic field is the pure-imaginary d=p half-turn; exceptional fields Q(i), Q(sqrt(-3)) do not admit norm 1/p nested solutions for p>3.
