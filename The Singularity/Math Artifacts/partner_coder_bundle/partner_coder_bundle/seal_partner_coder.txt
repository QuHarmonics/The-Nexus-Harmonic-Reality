SEALED PREDICTION - a coder that knows the partner. Dean A. Kulik, QuHarmonics Research Group, 2026-10-20.
Structure before data: in DNA the partner of a strand is its reverse complement (reverse the order, complement A<->T, C<->G), the same path as the ring's partner at a different location. A land-plant chloroplast genome carries two inverted repeats, one the reverse complement of the other: the half that builds itself. LZ-family coders (lzma) copy forward only, so they cannot use it. A coder whose match types include the partner can.
Data: NC_000932, the Arabidopsis thaliana chloroplast genome, as the GenBank record in the Biopython test suite (Tests/GenBank/NC_000932.gb); sequence = the letters of the ORIGIN section, uppercased. Controls: the same pass with forward copies only; and i.i.d. uniform random A/C/G/T of the same length from random.Random(20261020).
Coder: greedy pre-pass, k = 24 seed, minimum copy 32 bases. A reverse-complement copy token says S[i:i+L] = RC(S[s:s+L]) with s+L <= i; forward-copy tokens (control) say S[i:i+L] = S[j:j+L], j < i. Literals left over are coded by lzma (preset 9 extreme, ASCII bytes); tokens (literal run, distance, length) as varints, also lzma. Baseline: lzma of the whole sequence.
P1 (the partner is in the genome): reverse-complement copies cover at least 15% of NC_000932, and one copy is at least 20,000 bases long (the inverted repeat).
P2 (lzma cannot see it, the partner coder removes it): partner-coder size <= 0.87 x lzma(raw).
P3 (forward copies already belong to lzma): the forward-only pass gives >= 0.99 x lzma(raw).
P4 (no partner, no gain): on the random control the partner pass finds no copy of 32 or more bases and gives between 0.995 and 1.01 x lzma(raw).
P5 (exact): every encoding decodes to the original sequence, byte for byte.
Way home: partner_coder.py verifies this digest, fetches the record into its own directory, parses it, runs the coder and both controls, decodes everything, and prints P1-P5 with RUNS or DEAD END.
