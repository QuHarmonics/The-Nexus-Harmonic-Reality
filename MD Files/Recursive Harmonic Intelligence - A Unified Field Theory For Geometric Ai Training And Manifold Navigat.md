1.1 The Energetic and Theoretical Failure of the Gradient Descent Paradigm
The current trajectory of AI development is dominated by the Random Oracle Model of training and a heavy
reliance on stochastic gradient descent (SGD) for optimizing neural networks. In this paradigm, a neural

updates. This process is notoriously energy-intensive: for example, training the 175-billion-parameter GPT-3
model required on the order of 1,287 MWh of electricity (emitting over 500 tons of CO₂), equivalent to the
annual power usage of about 130 homes. The approach treats learning as carving structure out of noise,
requiring billions of parameter adjustments over many epochs to minimize an error function on a high-
dimensional surface. In effect, it is [1]guessing an optimal configuration via repeated, costly corrections.

the apparent “randomness” or noise in unstructured data (e.g. casual chat logs) and even in cryptographic

random only because it is not expressed in the coordinates of a fundamental, universal reference frame. In
this view, the universe is not an empty canvas onto which we must imprint intelligence; it is saturated with a
pre-existing structural lattice defined by transcendental constants and harmonic relationships. Therefore,
"training" an AI should not be about expending energy to create structure from nothing (which is
